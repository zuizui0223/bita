# BITA full-decoupling reachability certificate v1

## Purpose

Determine whether **any physically feasible decoupling intervention inside the registered coupling box** can possibly cross the static architecture boundary.

Earlier BITA results bracket the gain of a particular move and the minimum budget needed to cross. The present result adds a stronger reachability question:

> even if every registered residual-coupling channel were weakened as far as the model allows, could differentiation ever beat architecture cost?

This separates a merely underpowered intervention from a genuinely unreachable differentiated state within the registered architecture/coupling space.

## Setup

Let current residual coupling be

\[
\boldsymbol\lambda_0\ge0.
\]

A feasible decoupling move obeys the biological box

\[
0\le x\le\boldsymbol\lambda_0,
\]

so the fully decoupled endpoint inside the registered model is reached by

\[
x_{\rm full}=\boldsymbol\lambda_0.
\]

Let current active penalties be

\[
\mathbf c_0=-\nabla R(\boldsymbol\lambda_0)\ge0,
\]

and let current architecture deficit be

\[
\delta=K-R(\boldsymbol\lambda_0)>0.
\]

Assume throughout the full registered decoupling box/segments that

\[
\alpha I\preceq\nabla^2R\preceq\beta I,
\qquad 0\le\alpha\le\beta.
\]

Architecture cost `K` is held fixed for this certificate.

## Theorem 1 — global upper reachability bound

For any feasible `x`, second-order convexity gives

\[
\Delta R(x)
\le
\mathbf c_0^\top x+
\frac{\beta}{2}\|x\|_2^2.
\]

Because

\[
0\le x_i\le\lambda_{0i}
\]

and `c_0>=0`, we have

\[
\mathbf c_0^\top x
\le
\mathbf c_0^\top\boldsymbol\lambda_0
\]

and

\[
\|x\|_2^2
\le
\|\boldsymbol\lambda_0\|_2^2.
\]

Therefore every feasible intervention satisfies

\[
\boxed{
\Delta R(x)
\le
U_{\rm full}
:=
\mathbf c_0^\top\boldsymbol\lambda_0
+
\frac{\beta}{2}
\|\boldsymbol\lambda_0\|_2^2.
}
\]

## Corollary 1a — global impossibility certificate

If

\[
\boxed{U_{\rm full}\le\delta,}
\]

then for every feasible decoupling move

\[
R(\lambda_0-x)-K\le0.
\]

Thus

\[
\boxed{
\text{no registered decoupling intervention can reach the BITA side.}
}
\]

This is a global static **unreachability certificate** for the entire registered coupling box.

It is much stronger than saying that a proposed experimental manipulation is too weak.

## Theorem 2 — full-decoupling lower certificate

For the specific full-decoupling move `x_full=lambda_0`, the lower curvature bound gives

\[
\Delta R(x_{\rm full})
\ge
L_{\rm full}
:=
\mathbf c_0^\top\boldsymbol\lambda_0
+
\frac{\alpha}{2}
\|\boldsymbol\lambda_0\|_2^2.
\]

Hence if

\[
\boxed{L_{\rm full}>\delta,}
\]

then complete removal of all registered residual coupling is guaranteed to cross the static architecture boundary.

This proves **reachability**, though it does not prove that a cheaper partial intervention suffices.

## Theorem 3 — three-way reachability classification

The registered coupling architecture can therefore be classified as:

### `UNREACHABLE_WITHIN_REGISTERED_DECOUPLING`

if

\[
U_{\rm full}\le\delta.
\]

### `FULL_DECOUPLING_GUARANTEES_CROSSING`

if

\[
L_{\rm full}>\delta.
\]

### `REACHABILITY_UNRESOLVED_BY_CURVATURE_BOUNDS`

if

\[
L_{\rm full}\le\delta<U_{\rm full}.
\]

The unresolved band is generated by uncertainty about nonlinear recovery between the current point and full decoupling.

If `alpha=beta` and the bound is exact along the full path, the two full-decoupling bounds coincide and the reachability classification becomes exact.

## Corollary 3a — persistent BALANCE can have two mathematically distinct causes

A shared-favored state can persist because:

1. available decoupling interventions have not gone far enough even though a crossing is reachable; or
2. even the fully decoupled registered architecture cannot recover enough fitness to pay `K`.

The second case is qualitatively stronger. It says that **coupling relief alone cannot solve the conflict inside the registered architecture**, so a different architecture, lower `K`, altered baseline landscape, or another mechanism is required.

This is a BITA reachability statement, not a BALANCE direct-worldline classification and not a PAYOFF invasion result.

## Corollary 3b — full-decoupling ceiling without curvature lower bound

Even when only convexity (`alpha=0`) is trusted, the impossibility certificate remains valid whenever a credible finite `beta` upper bound exists.

Conversely, without an upper curvature bound, failure of the linear full-decoupling floor

\[
\mathbf c_0^\top\lambda_0
\]

to exceed `delta` does **not** prove unreachability, because positive curvature can add more recovery.

## Empirical consequence

A multi-channel BITA experiment can prospectively estimate or bound:

- the current coupling vector `lambda_0`;
- current active penalty vector `c_0`;
- curvature envelope `[alpha,beta]` over the manipulable coupling range;
- current architecture deficit `delta` on the matched fitness scale.

These yield a pre-experimental answer to a major design question:

```text
Is the available coupling range merely insufficiently sampled,
or is the BITA side mathematically unreachable within this architecture?
```

A system certified unreachable under one registered architecture becomes especially informative for the generality programme because it is a mechanistic boundary/negative case rather than a null result caused by weak manipulation.

## Claim ceiling

The certificate is only as broad as the registered coupling box. Unmeasured coupling channels, a new phenotype dimension, architecture redesign, or changes in `K` can invalidate the conclusion outside that box. The Hessian upper bound must hold over the whole feasible decoupling region used for the global impossibility claim. This remains a static optimized-fitness result; invasion and establishment belong to PAYOFF.
