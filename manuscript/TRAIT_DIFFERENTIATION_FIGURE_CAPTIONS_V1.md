# Figure captions — integrated trait-differentiation Chapter 2

## Figure 1. A trade-off can be resolved by compromise or by trait differentiation.

**A**, When two functions favour different states \(\theta_1\) and \(\theta_2\) of one shared trait \(z\), the optimized shared phenotype \(z^*\) is a compromise and retains an unavoidable shared-axis conflict loss \(L_S^*\). **B**, A differentiated architecture assigns the functions to distinct trait coordinates \(x\) and \(y\), but residual coupling \(\lambda\) can prevent the two optimized states from reaching the function-specific optima. The decoupling fraction \(s=|x^*-y^*|/|\theta_1-\theta_2|\) ranges from complete separation (\(s=1\)) toward integration as residual coupling increases. **C**, In the quadratic baseline the same \(s\) is the fraction of the shared conflict loss recoverable before the extra architecture cost \(K\) is charged, giving \(\Delta_{arch}=sL_S^*-K\). Differentiation is favoured only when the recoverable compromise loss exceeds the extra architecture cost. The figure describes optimized architecture states, not a historical transition path.

## Figure 2. Conflict load, decoupling and architecture cost jointly determine the differentiation boundary.

The quadratic decision boundary is \(K=sL_S^*\). Points below a given line favour the differentiated architecture; points above it favour the best shared compromise. A larger shared-axis conflict load makes more fitness available to recover, whereas stronger residual coupling decreases the decoupling fraction \(s\), lowering the maximum architecture cost that differentiation can pay. Lines for \(s=1.0,0.6,0.3\) are illustrative architecture states rather than empirical frequency estimates. Systems facing the same nominal trade-off can therefore resolve it differently if their attainable decoupling or additional architecture costs differ.

## Figure 3. The qualitative balance-to-differentiation result survives the declared nonquadratic robustness family and corresponds to observed partial architectures.

**A**, Registered finite design for convex power losses: functional powers \(p=1.5,2,3,4\), three functional-weight pairs, five residual-coupling values and five function-specific optimum distances, giving 300 evaluations at zero fixed architecture cost. **B**, All 300 nonzero-conflict evaluations had positive pre-cost recoverable conflict loss, and recoverable loss increased with optimum distance in all 60 declared fixed-shape/weight/coupling series. **C**, Increasing residual coupling never increased recoverable loss in all 60 declared fixed-shape/weight/distance series. These counts establish finite-family robustness, not universality across arbitrary fitness landscapes. **D**, Oral and pharyngeal jaws in cichlids illustrate structural/function partitioning with residual evolutionary and genetic integration, while comparative history in *Dalechampia* illustrates functional redeployment and addition of defensive lines. These empirical systems are architecture-state anchors only; they are not plotted as estimates of \(s\), \(\lambda\), \(K\) or \(\Delta_{arch}\).

## Figure 4. Once multiple trait axes exist, their joint fitness effect still requires mechanism identification.

The floral BITA system is used as a worked case for the second-stage inference problem. A focal pair of distinct traits \(A\) and \(D\) is first manipulated on a common biological outcome scale. Selective antagonist (\(G\)) and pollinator/mutualist (\(P\)) interventions are then crossed with the trait factorial. The \(A\times D\times G\times P\) term tests the separability of the consumer channels; a non-zero four-way interaction is itself evidence of cross-consumer coupling and prevents a single additive channel allocation. Consumer-independent reproduction or other baseline pathways must be measured rather than assumed to have zero \(A\times D\) interaction. Any remaining joint channel requires an independent assay before it is labelled as construction or allocation cost. Trait differentiation answers where functions are carried; this intervention sequence answers which causal pathway makes the resulting multi-trait architecture successful.

## Figure 5. Existing floral studies occupy complementary identification faces rather than a complete mechanism-allocation design.

Kessler et al. (2008) provides a rare manipulated attraction-by-defence-like reproductive factorial but lacks selective consumer toggles; Egan et al. (2021) provides a strong randomized herbivory-by-pollination environment but not an independently manipulated focal floral \(A\times D\); a public *Impatiens* reanalysis combines observational trait interactions with randomized consumer contexts but leaves the target interaction-modification intervals unresolved. Across the broader source-adjudicated synthesis, 56 route records from 25 independent biological clusters cover the constituent marginal pathway families, while 17 screened high-information systems occupy complementary portions of the identification design and none closes the full crossed allocation plus independent joint-channel assay. These results establish recurrence and fragmented identification, not the prevalence or historical origin of differentiated trait architectures.
