## Figure 1 — Higher robbery share can arise by different biological routes

Purpose: make the non-identifiability concrete before any dataset-specific result.

### Panel A — baseline

Use schematic counts only:

~~~text
legitimate = 90
robbery = 10
robbery share = 10%
~~~

### Panel B — numerator-driven increase

Keep legitimate use fixed and increase robbery:

~~~text
legitimate = 90
robbery = 45
robbery share = 33%
~~~

### Panel C — denominator loss

Let legitimate interaction collapse while robbery does not increase:

~~~text
legitimate = 10
robbery = 8
robbery share = 44%
~~~

The critical visual point is that robbery prevalence rises even though robbery itself
falls from 10 to 8.

### Panel D — Ecuadorian empirical decomposition

Show the post-open 1.8× effective-reach result:

~~~text
legitimate RR = 0.154 (0.087–0.271)
robbery RR = 0.816 (0.358–1.860)
robbery / legitimate RR = 5.31 (1.92–14.67)
~~~

Label the biological reading as strong loss of legitimate feeding with no detectable
increase in robbery. State directly that A–C are illustrative counts and D is the
observed Ecuadorian estimate; the 1.8× sensitivity is post-open and observational.

Main message:

> the same rise in robbery share can be generated by more robbery or by loss of
> legitimate interaction; zero-inclusive route-specific rates are required to tell
> which process occurred.
