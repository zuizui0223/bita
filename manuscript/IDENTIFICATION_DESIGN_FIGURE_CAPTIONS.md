# Figure captions — identification-design manuscript

**Figure 1. A total attraction-by-defence interaction does not identify its mechanism.** A two-level attraction (`A`) by defence (`D`) factorial directly estimates the discrete interaction `Delta_AD W = W11 - W10 - W01 + W00` on the chosen outcome scale. The same total interaction can be generated by different allocations among antagonist relief, pollinator interference, and a remaining joint channel. The inferential problem is therefore mechanism allocation, not detection of non-additivity.

**Figure 2. Crossed consumer interventions identify channel contrasts and test the separability assumption.** The proposed design crosses the same two-level `A × D` manipulation with antagonist and pollinator access, yielding 16 cells. Antagonist-exclusion contrasts provide the attraction-dependent antagonist-relief estimand (`rho_delta`), whereas pollinator-presence contrasts provide the pollinator-dependent increment in interference (`iota_increment_delta`). The latter becomes total `iota_delta` only after accounting for the pollinator-absent `A×D` baseline interaction (`m0_delta`). The apparent pollinator dependence of `rho_delta` and antagonist dependence of `iota_increment_delta` are the same `A×D×G×P` four-way contrast up to sign. A non-zero four-way term therefore rejects the simple separable channel representation rather than being absorbed into a channel estimate.

**Figure 3. Joint cost is independently assayed rather than defined by subtraction.** The crossed consumer experiment leaves an unallocated joint channel `U_delta = rho_delta - iota_delta - Delta_AD W`. This residual is not automatically interpreted as joint construction or allocation cost. A separate `A×D` assay under standardized or suppressed biotic pathways estimates the cost interaction (`kappa_assay_delta`). Agreement supports the intended allocation; disagreement diagnoses an omitted pathway, non-selective intervention, baseline misspecification, or scale mismatch. When `Delta_AD W > 0` but `rho_delta <= iota_delta`, the remaining joint channel must be negative; assigning that sign specifically to `kappa` still requires the independent assay.

**Figure 4. Existing high-information studies occupy complementary parts of the identification design.** Kessler et al. (2008) provides the closest experimentally crossed attraction-by-defence-like trait factorial: floral benzylacetone and nicotine production were crossed, and the published female-outcrossing aggregate implies a positive discrete interaction under the reported constraints, but selective antagonist and pollinator toggles are absent and nicotine silencing is systemic. Egan et al. (2021) provides the complementary consumer-factorial half by crossing herbivory and pollination environments, but the focal attraction and defence traits are measured rather than independently crossed. The lower panel shows the public-data retrofit of Soper Gorden and Adler (2018): observational `A×D` and its randomized robbing, florivory, and pollination modifiers can be estimated, but all eight target 95% intervals cross zero. Across the 16 screened high-information systems, none includes an independent joint-cost assay or full channel identification. Counts describe this screened design set, not literature prevalence.

**Figure 5. Experimental roadmap from interaction detection to mechanism identification.** An identifiable study begins with independently manipulable attraction and flower-specific defence coordinates, then selects a biological system that permits selective antagonist and pollinator interventions. The full `A×D×G×P` experiment estimates the total interaction and consumer contrasts; the pollinator-absent `A×D` baseline is measured rather than assumed zero; the four-way contrast tests channel separability; and an independent cost assay constrains the remaining joint channel. The experiment remains informative if separability fails or the residual and cost assay disagree, because those outcomes identify which causal representation is inadequate.
