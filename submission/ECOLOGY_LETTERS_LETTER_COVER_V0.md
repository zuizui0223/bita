# Ecology Letters Letter cover letter v1

**Article type:** Letter

**Provisional title:** Higher nectar robbery prevalence under access mismatch need not reflect higher robbery rates

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, “Higher nectar robbery prevalence under access mismatch need not reflect higher robbery rates,” as a Letter in *Ecology Letters*.

The manuscript asks a simple question that the standard conditional robbery response cannot answer: when access mismatch is associated with a higher probability of robbery among observed interactions, has robbery actually become more frequent, or have legitimate interactions been lost? We therefore reconstruct **zero-inclusive opportunities** and estimate legitimate and robbing feeding rates separately, rather than interpreting a change in conditional route composition as a change in absolute route use.

Our primary quantitative test uses an all-Ecuador bird–flower network. Across 259 plant species, mean tube–bill mismatch was positively associated with robbery share (rho=0.503, p=0.0001); the association persisted within bird species (1,285 bird × plant dyads; rho=0.340, p=0.0001) but not across bird-species means (rho=0.086, p=0.551). The preregistered exposed-culmen-only barrier instead gave a *positive* legitimate-feeding association (RR=1.883, 95% CI 1.111–3.190). We retain that original result while noting that bill length alone omits tongue extension and cannot establish an effective nectar-access barrier. Across three explicitly post-open hummingbird-only reach scenarios (4/3, 1.8, 2.0 times culmen), the separately fitted legitimate RR was consistently below 1 (0.354, 0.154, 0.100; all 95% CIs exclude 1). Robbery RRs were 0.851, 0.816, and 1.393, respectively; all 95% intervals included 1. Thus the sign-stable result **within these post-open scenarios** is a strong decline in legitimate use, not proof of an unchanged robbery rate. The descriptive ratio of separately fitted robbery-versus-legitimate RRs ranged 2.406–13.876 (at 1.8×, 5.31 [1.92–14.67]), but the 4/3× ratio CI includes 1. The models retain different positive-margin supports, so their ratio is not a single common-risk-set replacement estimate, and the per-scenario CIs do not include uncertainty over multiplier choice. No species-specific tongue-reach measurements validate a uniform multiplier across all 49 hummingbird species. The plant-level conditional prevalence result and opportunity-based rates are different estimands, not a causal numerical decomposition of robbery prevalence.

We then asked whether a smaller, independently assembled Afrotropical insect network recovered the same routing direction. Among 57 plant species, floral tube length was associated with a shift from nectar thieving toward robbing (rho=0.347, permutation p=0.0086). A source-defined multitrait sensitivity did not isolate tube length as a unique partial predictor, so we interpret this as corroboration of an access-geometry association rather than a unique tube-length effect.

The two datasets were never pooled at the observation level. Each contributes one plant-species rank effect with equal network weight, yielding rho_J=0.428 and permutation p=0.0001. Because the joint statistic contains only k=2 independent standardized networks, we do not estimate between-network heterogeneity or claim generality beyond these two systems. Separately, a third independent multispecies Hawaiian bird–lobelioid dataset published by Case et al. (2026) reports the same directional relationship between bill–flower mismatch and nectar robbery; because that direction was already public and its effect has not been harmonized to our network statistic, we treat it only as directional corroboration.

We do not claim that the component trait–robbery association, separate measurement of legitimate and robbing visitation rates, behavioral tactic switching, forbidden links, or ecological-network rewiring are new. Lara and Ornelas (2001), for example, experimentally manipulated corolla length and recorded separate legitimate and robbing visit counts, finding more robbing visits to long artificial corollas. Our contribution is narrower and network-scale: we start from a multispecies mismatch–robbery prevalence pattern and reconstruct zero-inclusive opportunities to estimate route-specific frequency associations rather than assuming the conditional fraction identifies the change in each route. In Ecuador, **higher robbery prevalence does not coincide with a detectable increase in robbery rate** under the literature-based effective-reach sensitivity; legitimate feeding instead falls sharply. This contrasts with prior numerator-driven cases and is consistent with the same prevalence pattern arising through different biological processes. The plant-level prevalence analysis and the route-specific fixed-effect models do not share one estimand or fitted support, so we do not claim to quantify how much of the prevalence association is generated by either absolute route. The within-bird-species result adds a relational trait-matching layer without identifying individual tactic changes, while the independent insect network establishes directional recurrence of routing rather than the same filtering mechanism. A frozen outcome-blind frame resolved 33 independent direct programs (22 positive, five null, four opposite and two mixed); these are used as boundary information, not as a success-rate or prevalence argument. In a separate post-hoc design audit of that frozen frame, only 7/33 programs met a strict zero-inclusive both-route-rate criterion; this finite-frame audit is not an estimate for the broader literature, but it shows why conditional robbery prevalence often cannot identify which absolute route changed.

All analyses and aggregate outputs are reproducible from public data and repository workflows.
