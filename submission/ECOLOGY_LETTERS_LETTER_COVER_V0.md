# Ecology Letters Letter cover letter v1

**Article type:** Letter

**Provisional title:** Higher nectar robbery prevalence under access mismatch need not reflect higher robbery rates

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, “Higher nectar robbery prevalence under access mismatch need not reflect higher robbery rates,” as a Letter in *Ecology Letters*.

The manuscript asks a simple question that the standard conditional robbery response cannot answer: when access mismatch is associated with a higher probability of robbery among observed interactions, has robbery actually become more frequent, or have legitimate interactions been lost? We therefore reconstruct **zero-inclusive opportunities** and estimate legitimate and robbing feeding rates separately, rather than interpreting a change in conditional route composition as a change in absolute route use.

Our primary quantitative test uses an all-Ecuador bird–flower network. Across 259 plant species, mean tube–bill mismatch was positively associated with robbery (rho=0.503, p=0.0001), and the signal persisted within bird species (1,285 bird × plant dyads; rho=0.340, p=0.0001) but not across bird-species means (rho=0.086, p=0.551). A separately frozen zero-inclusive model initially classified barriers using exposed culmen alone and suggested higher legitimate feeding under the barrier state. We retain that preregistered result, but a post-open physiological-reach audit shows that the participation inference depends on how the binary threshold represents effective reach. Restricting to hummingbirds and applying the 80% tongue-extension correction used in prior network work, legitimate feeding fell sharply under effective-reach barriers (RR=0.154, 95% CI 0.087–0.271), whereas robbery-only counts remained unresolved (0.816, 0.358–1.860); robbery was retained relative to legitimate feeding by a factor of 5.31 (1.92–14.67). The plant-level robbery contrast simultaneously strengthened to +0.259 (p=0.0001). Thus greater robbery share is consistent with selective filtering of the legitimate route rather than an absolute increase in robbery.

We then asked whether a smaller, independently assembled Afrotropical insect network recovered the same routing direction. Among 57 plant species, floral tube length was associated with a shift from nectar thieving toward robbing (rho=0.347, permutation p=0.0086). A source-defined multitrait sensitivity did not isolate tube length as a unique partial predictor, so we interpret this as corroboration of an access-geometry association rather than a unique tube-length effect.

The two datasets were never pooled at the observation level. Each contributes one plant-species rank effect with equal network weight, yielding rho_J=0.428 and permutation p=0.0001. Because the joint statistic contains only k=2 independent standardized networks, we do not estimate between-network heterogeneity or claim generality beyond these two systems. Separately, a third independent multispecies Hawaiian bird–lobelioid dataset published by Case et al. (2026) reports the same directional relationship between bill–flower mismatch and nectar robbery; because that direction was already public and its effect has not been harmonized to our network statistic, we treat it only as directional corroboration.

We do not claim that the component trait–robbery association, separate measurement of legitimate and robbing visitation rates, behavioral tactic switching, forbidden links, or ecological-network rewiring are new. The contribution is narrower: we start from a trait-mismatch network in which robbery prevalence is already associated with access geometry, then decompose that pattern with zero-inclusive route-specific rates. The primary result shows that **higher robbery prevalence need not imply a higher robbery rate**: under the literature-based effective-reach sensitivity, legitimate feeding is strongly reduced while robbery itself shows no detectable increase. The within-bird analysis further shows that route composition is relational rather than a fixed property of bird species, and the independent insect network establishes directional recurrence of routing rather than the same filtering mechanism. A frozen outcome-blind frame resolved 33 independent direct programs (22 positive, five null, four opposite and two mixed); these are used as boundary information, not as a success-rate or prevalence argument.

All analyses and aggregate outputs are reproducible from public data and repository workflows.
