# Cover letter draft — Theoretical Ecology

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“When are floral attraction and defence complementary? Mechanistic theory and meta-analytic patterns across mutualists and antagonists,”** for publication in *Theoretical Ecology*.

The manuscript is organized around a deliberately simple two-part question: **what mechanism determines the sign of the attraction–defence interaction, and what empirical Pattern recurs across systems?**

**Part I — Mechanism.** We develop a focal-trait mathematical framework around the mixed partial of a declared fitness or biological-outcome surface. We do not present the mixed partial itself as a new mathematical object. Instead, after an explicit orientation gate, the local interaction becomes a mechanistic balance among antagonist relief, interference with legitimate pollinators, and direct joint-cost curvature, `W_AD = rho - iota - kappa`. We show that exact knowledge of the total outcome surface does not identify those component mechanisms, derive the unrestricted environmental derivative balance, and identify the measurements required to distinguish them. An endpoint-normalized finite sensitivity analysis yields both complementary and substitutable regimes across 2,592 declared evaluations; these are theory sensitivity results, not estimates of natural prevalence.

**Part II — Pattern.** We then use the mechanism classes from Part I to organize cross-study evidence and ask which Patterns are recurrent, which are conditional, and which remain unidentified. Quantitative meta-analysis is used only where outcomes can be placed on defensible common scales. A random-effects reanalysis of Leal et al. (2025) shows recurrent reductions in female reproductive success, nectar standing crop, and legitimate visitation while retaining extreme among-study heterogeneity. A second 32-study-component synthesis of Sasidharan et al. (2023) shows shared floral-volatile responsiveness across pollinators and florivores but strong compositional and context dependence.

The broader source-adjudicated Pattern architecture is deliberately not pooled into a grand cross-outcome effect. After a registered saturation expansion, it contains **56 effect/directional records across 25 independent biological study clusters**, with all four mechanism families represented, **14 same-system multi-route clusters**, and **17 independent context/sign-switch clusters**. Seven additional context programs are tracked without being added to route-ledger N. The expansion broadens the empirical mechanism space beyond floral chemistry to visual and multidimensional attraction signals and to physically distinct flower-specific defences, including liquid barriers, sticky or slippery floral surfaces, petal hairs, and access-modifying bracts.

Several recurrent states are especially informative for the theory. Antagonist-reducing defences can occur without a detectable pollinator penalty on the tested response; access barriers can be gated by consumer size, attack mode, floral position, or reproductive stage; the same visitor can be routed between legitimate pollination and nectar robbery without changing its taxonomic identity or arrival rate; and one consumer taxon can reverse ecological role across its lifecycle. These Patterns make the observed heterogeneity mechanistically interpretable rather than merely residual variation.

Three additional published syntheses—Haas-Desmarais et al. (2026), Caruso et al. (2019), and Junker and Blüthgen (2010)—provide independent contextual support for tissue, consumer, trait-class, assay, and selection-environment dependence. We keep their evidence status and scales separate from the two reproduced quantitative modules rather than manufacturing a larger pooled analysis.

The central empirical result is therefore not a universal attraction–defence sign. Rather, the cross-system Pattern is **recurrent constituent mechanisms plus context-dependent balance**. Consistent with the theory’s identification requirements, a dedicated direct `A x D` search saturates with one strict sign-unresolved study cluster and a direct joint-cost search with no strict estimate, leaving `kappa` unidentified rather than zero.

We believe this Mechanism → Pattern architecture fits *Theoretical Ecology*: the mathematical part provides a general ecological principle and testable sign criterion, while the empirical part asks whether the mechanism-derived Patterns recur across real systems and shows why context dependence is a biological result rather than statistical noise. The empirical synthesis exposes generality and identification gaps without claiming that marginal literature effects calibrate the full mixed partial.

The manuscript is original and is not under consideration elsewhere. Code, declared configurations, source-adjudication products, quantitative synthesis readouts, saturation records, tests, and figure-generation workflows are maintained in the associated repository.
