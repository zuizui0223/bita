# Cover letter draft — Theoretical Ecology

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“When are floral attraction and defence complementary? A one-sided mechanistic bound and cross-system patterns,”** for publication in *Theoretical Ecology*.

The paper asks a simple ecological question: when does one focal floral attraction trait and one flower-specific antagonist-reducing trait become locally complementary rather than substitutable? Existing multivariate-selection and fitness-landscape approaches can describe total cross-trait curvature, but total curvature alone does not identify whether the interaction arises from antagonist relief, interference with legitimate pollinators, or direct joint cost.

**Part I — Mechanism.** After an explicit orientation gate, we organize the local mixed effect as `W_AD = rho - iota - kappa`, where `rho` is antagonist relief, `iota` pollinator interference, and `kappa` direct joint-cost curvature. The decomposition itself is bookkeeping. The stronger result is one-sided: under non-negative joint-cost curvature, complementarity can occur only where antagonist relief exceeds pollinator interference. Across 2,592 declared endpoint-normalized evaluations and four response-shape variants there are no counterexamples, while about 23% of in-window points remain substitutable. The selectivity window is therefore a necessary permissive region, not a universal two-sided sign criterion. A sufficiently negative joint-cost curvature is the unique escape route from this bound in the declared family.

**Part II — Pattern.** We then organize cross-study evidence by the mechanism classes defined in Part I. Quantitative meta-analysis is used only where compatible effect scales exist; otherwise source-adjudicated study clusters map recurrence, same-system linkage, switching, and identification gaps without manufacturing a grand cross-outcome effect. The saturated architecture contains 56 route-level records from 25 independent biological study clusters. Floral-larceny effects show that antagonist pressure can be costly on average (female-fitness log response ratio about -0.210; 48 clusters), yet only 35/48 effects are negative, the 95% prediction interval spans -1.13 to +0.71, and declared moderators explain only 0–8% of heterogeneity. Thus the exposure gate can be open while its realised magnitude and sign remain strongly system dependent.

The empirical synthesis is deliberately not presented as calibration of `rho`, `iota`, `kappa`, or total `W_AD`. Constituent mechanisms and switching architectures recur, but direct total attraction × defence evidence remains sparse. Direct joint-cost curvature has no strict empirical estimate in the admitted evidence layer. This final gap has a precise experimental interpretation: a 2 × 2 allocation design can test the sign of joint-cost curvature as a relatively inexpensive applicability/falsification gate, whereas a full attraction × defence factorial is required to estimate total interaction and channel allocation.

We believe this fits *Theoretical Ecology* because the manuscript uses a compact theoretical mechanism to derive an ecological impossibility boundary, then asks which components of that mechanism recur across real systems and what remains empirically identifiable. The paper does not claim that correlational selection, pollinator–antagonist nonadditivity, defence costs, or context dependence are new phenomena. Its contribution is the one-sided mechanistic bound, the explicit failure mode, and a theory-structured empirical synthesis that turns the remaining uncertainty into a concrete falsification and calibration programme.

The manuscript is original and is not under consideration elsewhere. Code, declared configurations, source-adjudication products, quantitative-synthesis readouts, saturation records, regression tests, and figure-generation workflows are maintained in the associated repository. Final author approval, archival DOI, repository licence, and portal-controlled metadata must be completed before this letter is used for actual submission.
