# Cover letter draft — Theoretical Ecology

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“When are floral attraction and defence complementary? A local mechanistic diagnostic and mechanism-pattern synthesis across mutualists and antagonists,”** for publication in *Theoretical Ecology*.

Animal-pollinated flowers must attract mutualists while remaining exposed to florivores, nectar robbers, pathogens, and other antagonists. Existing work has established multivariate floral selection, interaction modification, and numerous effects of floral signals and defence on pollinators and antagonists. The unresolved inferential problem is narrower: for one explicitly defined attraction trait and one flower-specific antagonist-reducing defence or access trait, when does either trait increase rather than decrease the local marginal return to the other, and which ecological channel produces that interaction?

We develop a focal-trait framework around the mixed partial of a declared fitness or biological-outcome surface. We do not present the mixed partial itself as a new mathematical object. Instead, after an explicit orientation gate, the local interaction is expressed as a balance among antagonist relief, interference with legitimate pollinators, and direct joint-cost curvature, `W_AD = rho - iota - kappa`. We show that exact knowledge of the total fitness surface does not identify those component mechanisms, derive the unrestricted environmental derivative balance, and state the measurements required to distinguish them. An endpoint-normalized finite sensitivity analysis then demonstrates both complementary and substitutable regimes across 2,592 declared evaluations without interpreting those grid occupancies as frequencies in nature.

The manuscript adds a theory-driven empirical synthesis rather than treating a single marginal pathway as validation of the model. The source-adjudicated architecture records all four theory-facing marginal mechanism families, ten same-system multi-route study clusters, and eleven independent context/sign-switch clusters. A dedicated direct `A x D` search reaches its registered stopping rule with one strict, sign-unresolved study, while a separate direct joint-cost search reaches its stopping rule with no strict estimate; `kappa` is therefore carried as empirically unidentified rather than set to zero.

Two biologically distinct quantitative deposited-synthesis modules provide independent cross-study evidence for constituent mechanisms. Reanalysis of Leal et al. (2025) shows substantial realised costs of floral larceny for nectar standing crop, legitimate visitation, and female reproductive success, while retaining extreme among-study heterogeneity and sensitivity diagnostics. Reanalysis of Sasidharan et al. (2023) recovers a conservative 32-study-component floral-volatile synthesis in which pollinator and florivore responses show shared tracking but also repeated context-dependent disagreement. Neither module is presented as an empirical estimate of `W_AD`; their role is to establish mechanism recurrence and conditionality while preserving the identification boundary.

We believe the manuscript is suitable for *Theoretical Ecology* because the theoretical result and empirical synthesis are deliberately coupled: theory identifies the sign boundary and the missing measurements, while the evidence map shows which constituent channels recur in real systems, where their expression changes with context, and where direct identification remains absent. The paper therefore ends with a concrete factorial design for estimating the total interaction and its mechanism-specific curvatures in the same biological units.

The manuscript is original, is not under consideration elsewhere, and all authors have approved its submission. Code, declared configurations, source-adjudication products, tests, and derived outputs are maintained in the associated repository.
