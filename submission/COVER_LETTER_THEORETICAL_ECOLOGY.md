# Cover letter draft — Theoretical Ecology

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“When are floral attraction and defence complementary? Mechanistic theory and meta-analytic patterns across mutualists and antagonists,”** for publication in *Theoretical Ecology*.

The manuscript is organized around a deliberately simple two-part question: **what mechanism determines the sign of the attraction–defence interaction, and what empirical pattern recurs across systems?**

**Part I — Mechanism.** We develop a focal-trait mathematical framework around the mixed partial of a declared fitness or biological-outcome surface. We do not present the mixed partial itself as a new mathematical object. Instead, after an explicit orientation gate, the local interaction becomes a mechanistic balance among antagonist relief, interference with legitimate pollinators, and direct joint-cost curvature, `W_AD = rho - iota - kappa`. We show that exact knowledge of the total outcome surface does not identify those component mechanisms, derive the unrestricted environmental derivative balance, and identify the measurements required to distinguish them. An endpoint-normalized finite sensitivity analysis yields both complementary and substitutable regimes across 2,592 declared evaluations; these are theory sensitivity results, not estimates of natural prevalence.

**Part II — Pattern.** We then use the mechanism classes from Part I to organize cross-study evidence and ask which patterns are recurrent, which are conditional, and which remain unidentified. Quantitative meta-analysis is used only where outcomes can be placed on defensible common scales. A random-effects reanalysis of the Leal et al. (2025) floral-larceny dataset shows recurrent reductions in female reproductive success, nectar standing crop, and legitimate visitation, while retaining extreme among-study heterogeneity. A second 32-study-component quantitative synthesis of Sasidharan et al. (2023) shows shared floral-volatile responsiveness across pollinators and florivores but strong compositional and context dependence. A separate source-adjudicated evidence map links these quantitative patterns to all four theory-facing mechanism families, ten same-system multi-route clusters, and eleven independent context/sign-switch clusters without manufacturing a cross-outcome grand effect.

The central empirical result is therefore not a universal attraction–defence sign. Rather, the cross-system pattern is **recurrent constituent mechanisms plus context-dependent balance**: the processes required by the theory recur, but dose, resources, exposure, consumer identity, response definition, and compound identity repeatedly change which channel is expressed. Consistent with the theory’s identification requirements, a dedicated direct `A x D` search saturates with one strict sign-unresolved study and a direct joint-cost search with no strict estimate, leaving `kappa` unidentified rather than zero.

We believe this Mechanism → Pattern architecture fits *Theoretical Ecology*: the mathematical part provides a general ecological principle and testable sign criterion, while the meta-analytic part asks whether the mechanism-derived patterns recur across real systems and shows why context dependence is a biological result rather than statistical noise. The journal explicitly seeks theoretical approaches that answer ecological questions and are readable by a broad ecological audience. The empirical synthesis is used to expose general patterns and identification gaps, not to claim that marginal literature effects calibrate the full mixed partial.
