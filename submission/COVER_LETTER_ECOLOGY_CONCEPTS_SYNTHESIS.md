# Cover letter — Ecology, Concepts & Synthesis

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“From floral trait interactions to mechanism identification: a crossed-intervention framework for attraction and defence,”** for publication as a **Concepts & Synthesis** article in *Ecology*.

The manuscript addresses a common ecological inference problem: a trait interaction can be detected on fitness while the ecological pathways that generated it remain incompletely identified. Floral attraction and defence provide a tractable case because the same traits can alter pollination, antagonist damage, reproductive assurance, and direct allocation or construction channels. We therefore distinguish **interaction detection**, **partial identification**, and **point mechanism allocation**.

The central contribution is an operational identification framework. The primary estimand is a directly measurable two-level attraction-by-defence interaction, `Delta_AD`. A measured total interaction defines a set of compatible channel allocations rather than a unique mechanism. Explicit biological or channel bounds can shrink that set before a fully crossed experiment is available. In particular, `kappa_delta >= 0` implies `rho_delta - iota_delta >= Delta_AD W`, recovering the earlier one-sided relation as a partial-identification bound rather than a standalone theorem; the bound is assumption-indexed through the stated kappa restriction.

For point identification, a crossed `A × D × antagonist × pollinator` experiment isolates antagonist-relief and pollinator-dependent contrasts only when consumer interventions are selective and the same trait coordinates are maintained across cells. The design tests its own separability assumption: pollinator-dependence of antagonist relief and antagonist-dependence of pollinator interference are the same four-way interaction up to sign. Pollinator-independent reproduction is measured rather than assumed away, and any remaining joint channel is kept unallocated until an independent attraction-by-defence assay constrains its interpretation.

The cross-system synthesis provides a separate Pattern layer rather than validation of the algebra. Across 56 source-adjudicated route records from 25 independent biological clusters, all four constituent marginal pathway families recur, including same-system and context-switching architectures. These records establish recurrence, not `rho_delta`, `iota_delta`, `Delta_AD W`, `kappa_delta`, or natural prevalence.

The stricter empirical audit reveals a **fragmented identification frontier**. Kessler et al. (2008) supplies the closest trait-factorial anchor; Egan et al. (2021) supplies the complementary consumer-factorial structure; a Dryad-backed *Impatiens capensis* reanalysis reaches randomized modification of an observational attraction-by-defence association; and *Pedicularis rex* illustrates a selective-defence system. Across 16 screened high-information systems, no study closes all allocation dimensions or contains an independent joint-cost assay. The informative result is therefore not simply `0/16`: existing experiments already constrain complementary parts of the problem, which makes the next measurement or intervention study-specific.

We believe this fits *Ecology*'s Concepts & Synthesis section because it turns a recurring problem of ecological context dependence into an executable inference sequence: **interaction detection → partial identification → mechanism identification**. The framework remains useful whether the final interaction is positive, negative, non-separable, or inconsistent with the independent joint-channel assay, because each outcome identifies a different limitation of the proposed causal representation.
