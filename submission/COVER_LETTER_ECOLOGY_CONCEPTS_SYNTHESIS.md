# Cover letter — Ecology, Concepts & Synthesis

Dear Editors,

Please consider our manuscript, **“From floral trait interactions to mechanism identification: a crossed-intervention framework for attraction and defence,”** for publication as a **Concepts & Synthesis** article in *Ecology*.

The manuscript addresses a common ecological inference problem: a trait interaction can be detected on fitness while the ecological pathways that generated it remain unidentified. Floral attraction and defence provide a tractable case because the same traits can alter pollination, antagonist damage, reproductive assurance, and direct allocation or construction costs. We therefore distinguish **interaction detection** from **mechanism allocation** and ask what additional experiment is required to move from one to the other.

The central contribution is an operational identification framework. The primary estimand is a directly measurable two-level attraction-by-defence interaction, `Delta_AD`. A crossed `A × D × antagonist × pollinator` experiment then identifies antagonist-relief and pollinator-dependent contrasts only when the consumer interventions are selective and the same trait coordinates are maintained across cells. The design also tests its own separability assumption: the apparent pollinator-dependence of antagonist relief and antagonist-dependence of pollinator interference are the same four-way interaction up to sign. Pollinator-independent reproduction is measured rather than assumed away, and any remaining joint channel is kept unallocated until an independent attraction-by-defence cost assay constrains its interpretation.

The simple algebra linking total interaction and channel contrasts is deliberately not presented as the novelty. Its role is diagnostic after the relevant contrasts have been measured. For example, observed complementarity together with antagonist relief no greater than pollinator interference forces a negative remaining joint channel on the declared scale; attributing that channel specifically to joint cost still requires the independent assay.

We stress-test the framework against high-information published systems rather than treating marginal pathway studies as validation. Kessler et al. (2008) supplies the closest trait-factorial anchor by experimentally crossing floral benzylacetone and nicotine production; published aggregate constraints imply a sign-robust positive discrete reproductive interaction, although formal interaction uncertainty and systemic nicotine effects remain limitations. Egan et al. (2021) supplies the complementary consumer-factorial structure by crossing herbivory and pollination while the focal traits are not independently manipulated. A Dryad-backed reanalysis of *Impatiens capensis* reaches randomized modification of an observational attraction-by-defence association but not channel identification. Across a screened set of 16 high-information systems, no study combines the trait factorial, selective consumer interventions, pollinator-absent baseline characterization, and an independent joint-cost assay. This is a coverage result for the screened set, not an estimate of literature prevalence.

We believe this fits *Ecology*'s Concepts & Synthesis section because it turns a recurring problem of ecological context dependence into an executable measurement design. The framework is useful whether the final interaction is positive, negative, non-separable, or inconsistent with the independent cost assay: each outcome identifies a different limitation of the proposed causal representation.
