# Prospective Cape third-network collaboration inquiry v1

**Status:** send-ready draft; explicit author send approval still required.

**Subject:** Prospective small-mammal × Protea study of floral access routing

Dear Prof Steenhuisen and Prof Midgley,

My name is Ruiqi Zhang. I am developing a preregistered comparative study of how floral access constraints change the route by which animal visitors obtain nectar.

Two independently assembled public networks—one insect and one hummingbird network—currently recover the same rank-level prediction: as the legitimate floral access route becomes more mechanically constrained, bypass use becomes more common. The present limitation is that this has only been tested in two visitor faunas.

I have therefore frozen a prospective third-network protocol before collecting any new outcome data. The proposed third system is a non-flying small-mammal × Protea network in the Cape. The analysis is already fixed as:

- access mismatch: log(plant legitimate nectar-access depth / mammal functional rostral reach);
- outcome: bypass events / (bypass + legitimate events);
- inferential unit: mammal species × Protea species × site;
- minimum eligibility: 5 mammal species, 5 plant species and 30 matched units, with a planning target of at least 70 units;
- route coding from infrared video, blind to plant and mammal morphology.

I am contacting you because your work has established both the small-mammal visitor diversity and the remote-camera feasibility of Cape Protea systems.

To preserve the prospective test, I am deliberately **not** asking which species or sites have shown more destructive/bypass behavior in previous observations. At this stage I only want to identify a route-blind feasible field system.

Would you be interested in discussing a collaboration, or advising whether there is a Cape site or coordinated set of nearby sites where the following could realistically be achieved?

1. at least five flowering Protea species sampled under one coordinated programme;
2. route-blind evidence for at least five non-flying mammal visitor species;
3. near-focus infrared camera deployment at inflorescences;
4. measurement of legitimate nectar-access depth;
5. access to suitable regional mammal rostral morphometrics, or a feasible non-invasive/approved route to obtain them;
6. a clear land-access, permitting and ethics pathway.

The site and plant list would be frozen before confirmatory route videos are opened, and pilot route frequencies would not be used to select sites. If the eventual mammal result is null or opposite in sign, it remains the confirmatory third test.

I can share the full preregistration, route-coding manual, field-readiness gate and reproducible analysis pipeline. I have also prepared a short route-blind feasibility form that asks only about candidate sites, flowering species, mammal presence, camera logistics, morphology sources and permission/ethics pathways.

To preserve the confirmatory design, please do not include unpublished site-, plant- or mammal-specific information about which taxa show more destructive/lateral/bypass access, route-specific counts, or route-specific coefficients before we freeze the candidate site and plant list.

I would be very grateful for any advice on whether this is practical in the Cape system and, if so, where a route-blind feasibility survey should begin.
