## Submission route

### Route A — Ecology Letters Synthesis proposal first

Current official journal guidance states that Ecology Letters prioritizes clearly stated ecological hypotheses, broad novelty and general interest. Its Synthesis format can combine literature synthesis, quantitative meta-analysis and modelling, with a maximum of 7,500 words and 10 display items. Unsolicited Synthesis submissions are proposal-gated before full submission.

Official source:
https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/page/journal/14610248/homepage/ForAuthors.html

Why BITA can plausibly fit:

- the paper is hypothesis-driven rather than descriptive;
- it derives a general threshold principle rather than only summarizing floral-defence cases;
- evidence is triangulated across case, within-system and community scales;
- two independent network reanalyses add quantitative cross-fauna tests rather than a review-only narrative;
- the principle is potentially transferable to other mutualist–antagonist access conflicts.

Main editorial risk:

- strict matched-D Stage-2 inference remains only n=3;
- the broad principle must therefore be sold through cross-scale convergence, not through a claim of completed meta-regression;
- the proposal must make the general ecological principle larger than the floral study system without pretending that the present data already test other systems.

Action:

```text
send short Synthesis proposal
do not reformat the full paper first
```

### Route B — Functional Ecology Research Article

Functional Ecology currently states that it publishes work that significantly advances mechanistic understanding of ecological pattern and process and accepts diverse approaches from organismal to community scale. Its author guidelines explicitly accommodate research papers using data from multiple published sources, including meta-analysis-style Data Sources sections.

Official sources:
https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/hub/journal/13652435/aims-and-scope/read-full-aims-and-scope
https://besjournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/hub/journal/13652435/author-guidelines

Why this is the cleanest full-submission fit:

- mechanistic ecological question;
- plant–visitor interactions;
- theory + source-adjudicated comparative synthesis + public-data analysis;
- standard Introduction / Methods / Results / Discussion architecture already exists;
- current length is comfortably below the point where >7,500-word justification is required.

Required adaptation:

- Functional Ecology uses a numbered factual abstract style;
- add an explicit replication statement;
- add a formal Data Sources section for matched-D primary sources;
- make the mechanistic prediction and the cross-fauna Sakhalkar + Aubert/EPHI network recurrence the first two selling points in the cover letter.

### Route C — Oikos

Oikos currently emphasizes ecological mechanisms, processes and patterns and explicitly publishes Research, Forum and Meta-analysis contributions.

Official source:
https://nsojournals.onlinelibrary.wiley.com/journal/16000706

Why it fits:

- strong theory–pattern bridge;
- acceptable mix of conceptual and empirical ecology;
- community-scale interaction-routing result is directly in scope;
- explicit Meta-analysis route provides a natural home if the matched-system synthesis is formalized further.

Current limitation:

- the strict matched-D component is a structured comparative synthesis rather than a conventional pooled-effect meta-analysis.

Therefore Oikos is cleaner as a general Research paper unless the quantitative matched-D lane expands materially.

### Route D — New Phytologist presubmission only

New Phytologist spans plant interactions and ecology from organismal to global scales and welcomes presubmission enquiries.

Official source:
https://www.newphytologist.org/journals/new-phytologist

The biological topic is in scope, but the present manuscript is a mixed synthesis + reanalysis rather than a conventional new-data plant research paper. New Phytologist remains plausible only if an editor positively responds to a presubmission enquiry emphasizing the new general plant-interaction principle.

## What not to change for journal targeting

Do not:

- inflate strict Stage-2 n;
- relabel null-compatible outcomes as preservation;
- claim domain > modality;
- remove the 14,383 vs 14,391 deposited-data discrepancy;
- convert Sakhalkar association into a causal experiment;
- discard the legacy BITA identification framework.

Journal targeting should change emphasis and packaging, not scientific claim ceilings.
