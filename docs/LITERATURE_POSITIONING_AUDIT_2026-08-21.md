# Literature positioning audit — ecological interpretation and simple-bound framing

Date: 2026-08-21

Purpose: targeted literature-positioning audit for the frozen Ecology — Concepts & Synthesis manuscript. This is **not** a reopening of broad Pattern evidence discovery. It asks whether the current Introduction and Discussion (i) correctly position the attraction–defence problem relative to close prior work, (ii) keep ecological interpretations within what the empirical literature supports, and (iii) make clear that the algebra is elementary while the ecological contribution is the one-sided exclusion, failed converse, and falsification gate.

## 1. Close prior art that must be acknowledged

The component biology is well established and should be treated as prior art:

- Strauss & Whittall (2006): non-pollinator agents can impose direct or indirect selection on floral traits; floral antagonists and pollinators can impose conflicting selection.
- Adler (2008): explicitly reviews selection by pollinators and herbivores on attraction and defence, including whether resistance/attraction and herbivores/pollinators are independent.
- Kessler & Halitschke (2009): develops a conceptual framework for conflicting selection on floral chemical traits, including defence costs, pollinator deterrence, and tissue-specific mechanisms that can reduce conflict.
- Johnson, Campbell & Barrett (2015): reviews evolutionary interactions between plant reproduction and herbivore defence; reproductive traits and defence can be interdependent through selection and allocation.
- Lucas-Barbosa (2016): argues that herbivore-induced defence and pollination ecology must be integrated within the same systems and linked to plant fitness; mechanisms and resource allocation need direct study.
- Rusman, Lucas-Barbosa & Poelman (2018): experimentally shows specificity in herbivore–flower-visitor interactions, with responses depending on herbivore feeding mode/site and visitor identity.
- Sletvold (2019): reviews context dependence of pollinator-mediated selection and emphasizes factorial manipulation of pollination together with other selective factors.
- Catford et al. (2022): distinguishes mechanistic from apparent context dependence and argues that ecological theory should structure interaction effects so that context dependence becomes explainable and generalizable rather than a label for heterogeneity.

These studies mean the manuscript must **not** claim novelty for: joint pollinator–herbivore effects, attraction–defence conflict, context dependence, resource/allocation costs, or modelling multiple partners.

## 2. Remaining narrow gap

The defensible gap is narrower:

> close prior work describes conflicting/reinforcing selection, ecological costs, context dependence, and evolutionary outcomes, but does not by itself isolate a one-sided exclusion for the focal local attraction × defence mixed curvature under an explicit joint-cost sign premise.

The paper therefore asks a deliberately limited question before prediction:

> can we identify a region of mechanism space in which local complementarity is impossible?

The algebraic answer is elementary once the channels are written down. The contribution is not mathematical difficulty; it is ecological compression:

1. separate antagonist relief, pollinator interference, and direct joint-cost curvature;
2. prove that non-negative joint-cost curvature excludes complementarity outside `rho > iota`;
3. show the converse fails when joint cost is positive;
4. identify sufficiently negative joint-cost curvature as the escape route;
5. turn the remaining uncertainty into an ordered falsification/calibration programme.

## 3. Ecological interpretation audit

### Strongly supported interpretations

- Attraction can expose flowers to antagonists as well as mutualists.
- Defence or herbivore-induced responses can alter pollinator behaviour and reproductive outcomes.
- Effects depend on consumer identity, feeding mode, attack geometry, plant stage, and ecological context.
- Context dependence often reflects interaction effects rather than one universal mean effect.

### Supported as interpretation, not direct parameter estimates

- The selectivity window can be interpreted biologically as **functional discrimination**: ecological arrangements that increase antagonist relief relative to pollinator interference (e.g. tissue specificity, attack-mode filtering, guarded barriers, spatial/temporal separation).
- Existing examples demonstrate that such route separation is biologically realizable, but they do not directly estimate `rho - iota` and therefore do not classify systems as mathematically inside the selectivity window.

### Hypothesis-generating only

- Shared resource, construction, allocation, biochemical, or developmental constraints are plausible biological sources of `kappa`.
- Existing work documents defence costs, reproductive allocation effects, pleiotropy, and resource-reallocation hypotheses, but the frozen strict audit found no direct estimate of the additional joint cost of simultaneous investment in distinct focal attraction and flower-specific defence axes.
- Therefore these mechanisms motivate candidate measurements of `kappa`; they do not identify it.

## 4. “Complex-looking but simple” communication standard

The manuscript should say explicitly that:

> the derivation is intentionally elementary. Once the interaction is written as relief minus interference minus joint cost, the one-sided bound follows in one line. The scientific value lies in identifying which ecological partition makes that elementary inequality informative, what it rules out, why the converse fails, and which missing quantity can falsify applicability.

This should appear at four reader checkpoints:

1. Abstract — “the algebra is elementary; the ecological consequence is one-sided.”
2. Introduction — distinguish mathematical simplicity from ecological inference value.
3. Immediately after Theorem 1 — provide a plain-language translation.
4. Discussion — return to “simple exclusion, complex realization”: the bound is simple, while ecological context moves the component channels.
