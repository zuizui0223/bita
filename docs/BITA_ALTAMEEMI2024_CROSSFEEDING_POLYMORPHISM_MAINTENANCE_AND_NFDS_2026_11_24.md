## Result first: a new *maintenance*, not de novo role-splitting, anchor

Original source: Al-Tameemi Z & Rodríguez-Verdugo A (2024), *mSystems*, [DOI 10.1128/msystems.01053-24](https://doi.org/10.1128/msystems.01053-24), [original published article](https://journals.asm.org/doi/10.1128/msystems.01053-24), [PMC11575400](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11575400/).

The complete original full-text JATS was acquired from [Europe PMC's primary article archive](https://www.ebi.ac.uk/europepmc/webservices/rest/PMC11575400/fullTextXML) and authenticated against the original DOI and the registered Results/methodological caveats. **SHA-256 `f264ccad7af897c9836ac1144ffc5d8cbdede97a034684ec7d2b9b799b223af0`**, 134,602 original XML bytes. The raw peer-reviewed full text is **not redistributed**; only a source-grain and analysis receipt is frozen.

In the original experiment, **four co-culture and four monoculture `Pseudomonas putida` populations were propagated for roughly 200 generations**. The co-culture condition included the cross-feeding species `Acinetobacter johnsonii`, which consumes benzyl alcohol and releases benzoate used by `P. putida`.

| Observation | Independently evolved P. putida populations | Correct inference |
|---|---:|---|
| Co-culture with A. johnsonii | **4/4** with both small and large colony morphotypes at generation 200 | Retained phenotypic polymorphism under this original community regime |
| Monoculture | **0/4** with both morphotypes at generation 200 | Single, larger colony morphotype at endpoint; **large morphotype mutations did arise in monoculture before fixing** |

**This is a persistence/retention contrast, not a 4/4 vs 0/4 difference in the first appearance of novel genotypes.** The source traced only one replicate (number 2) longitudinally: large morphotype first arose around 40 generations under monoculture and 70 under co-culture, then coexisted to generation 200 in co-culture, whereas monoculture's small type was eventually lost around generation 140. The other lineages are source-supported endpoint morphotype classifications, *not another four full daily lineage trajectories*.

The four experimental source line ancestors descended from single clones with minor divergence. Colonies assayed from those lines (30 per line), the growth-curve replicates, mutation calls and source passage times are nested observations and must never be counted as more than the corresponding four evolved populations per treatment.

## Small-sample exact sensitivity: design assumptions matter

For the original **4/4 versus 0/4** endpoint 2×2 source table, exact post-publication calculations give:

- **Absolute observed difference** in the fraction of populations retaining both morphotypes: **+1.00**, not a general evolutionary risk difference outside these matched laboratory conditions.
- Unpaired two-sided Fisher exact reference: **2/70 = 0.02857**, requiring the premise that the four co-culture and four monoculture founder lines can be treated as independent across treatment arms.
- If instead the corresponding replicate numbers are viewed as **four paired source-founder blocks**, the conditional exact McNemar/sign reference from four discordant pairs is **2/16 = 0.125**.

These are **alternative exploratory reference calculations under distinct dependence assumptions**, not two confirmatory tests from eight independent biological histories. The original study used four line-specific ancestral clones per species; published details do not make the shared founder structure equivalent to a fully randomized matched-block trial. Consequently the observation is stronger than the evidence for a *p-threshold-dependent claim* of universal origin/maintenance probability.

## Negative mechanism test: coexistence does not certify classical NFDS

The original source assessed **a selected fleQ-mutant / small-colony clone pair from one co-culture replicate (#2)** across starting frequencies in the presence and absence of the cross-feeding species. It **did not detect the expected mutual invasion/negative frequency-dependent selection signature** in that two-clone assay; the published pooled genotype-relative-fitness test of partner presence had **df=28 and p=0.814**.

**Permitted:** the tested two-genotype focal pair and source assay did *not support* the negative-frequency-dependence mechanism expected to explain observed population-level coexistence.

**Not permitted:** negative frequency dependence is absent from all four co-cultures; the microbial system has no resource-mediated spatial/demographic processes; the original author's "no significant difference" proves equivalence or a strict zero selection effect. The two clones do not exhaust the genetic diversity present in the full evolving line, and a nonsignificant result has limited power.

This is an **informative unresolved-mechanism contrast** to the separately sourced Pineau 2024 oxygen-dependent PO-4 pair, where source cell-level competition data showed a negative frequency-dependent equilibrium under standard oxygen. A collective outcome (multiple morphotypes remaining) does **not automatically uniquely identify the consumer/resource/competition mechanism**.

## One-history replay, not six newly evolved founder lines

The authors revived **one original evolution co-culture line (#2) frozen at approximately 138 generations**, then split it into partner-present and partner-removed conditions and continued for approximately 50 generations. Figure 7 displays **six technical cultures per condition**, all descended from this *same original evolving community*. The source reported that both morphotypes remained present even without `A. johnsonii`; however, the Small type reached about **four times** the density of the Large type, whereas the partner-present community showed comparatively buffered/stable relative densities.

The experiment therefore **does not show extinction** of the second morphotype upon partner removal over the short replay. It illustrates **change in composition and reduced dominance**, and the replay six tubes are **technical descendants of one original population**, not six independent full-history replication events.

## Genotype/trait evidence and a decisive resource confound

Source-reported population/genetic and functional analyses find that mutations in flagellar/biofilm regulators, including `fleQ` and `flgH`, accompany improved bacterial yield but reduced motility and biofilm. In source replicate #2, the Large type achieved approx. **0.068 OD/mM**, compared with **0.020 OD/mM** for the ancestor (~**3.4×**) and **0.023 OD/mM** for the small type. These are from the source-specific measured phenotypes, *not* a shared common fitness scale for two jointly maintained tasks or an engineered versus original architectural comparison.

**Major alternative explanation explicitly acknowledged by the authors:** in the monoculture evolution condition, benzoate was **not externally supplemented** to match the by-product supplied by `A. johnsonii` in co-culture. Carbon availability and population demography may therefore differ alongside biotic interactions. This is not a clean partner-presence-only causal intervention for initial 200-generation persistence. The replay removal assay likewise does not make all abiotic resource differences vanish.

The 2026 800-generation related `P. putida/A. johnsonii` paper by overlapping authors is **not** imported as a separate independent research programme without a demonstrated nonoverlapping set of starting lineage histories. The 2024 paper also analyzes a 200-generation experiment originally described in prior work by Rodríguez-Verdugo & Ackermann; that prior source is not a newly started 2024 programme.

### Manuscript-ready short Results/Discussion paragraph (historical Chapter 2 / SLK provenance only)

> Across twelve selectively assembled experimental research programmes, the persistence of differentiated phenotypes proved distinguishable from their evolutionary appearance, collective productivity, and genetic reversal. In one independently evolved bacterial system, all four `Pseudomonas putida` populations maintained with the resource-supplying `Acinetobacter johnsonii` retained both large and small colony morphotypes after 200 generations, whereas none of four monoculture populations retained that bimodality (Al-Tameemi & Rodríguez-Verdugo, 2024). The large morphotype nevertheless emerged during monoculture evolution, indicating a difference in retention rather than a simple absence of evolutionary origins. Crucially, a reciprocal-invasion test of two representative genotypes did not establish negative frequency-dependent selection, even though the full co-culture population maintained morphological diversity. A later replay from one evolving source community showed altered relative morphotype densities after removing the partner but no complete loss of either morphotype. These observations complement cases of oxygen-dependent coexistence in multicellular yeast and evolved loss of specialist relationships in microbial consortia, but leave both the full-community maintenance mechanism and a historical ancestral two-function module split unresolved.
