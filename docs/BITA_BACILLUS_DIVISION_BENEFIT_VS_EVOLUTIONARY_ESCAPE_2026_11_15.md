# BITA: Short-term beneficial division of labour versus evolutionary autonomy in Bacillus

Date: 2026-11-15. Empirical lane: one Bacillus EPS/TasA experimental programme with a 2018 short-term paper and a related 2018 long-term follow-up. This is an original public mutation-supplement reanalysis plus source-indexed original productivity and follow-up narrative, **not** two independent evolved trait origins and not a pooled meta-analysis.

## 1. Same-ancestor architecture comparison: beneficial over WT under an appropriate context

Dragoš et al. (2018), *Current Biology*, DOI **10.1016/j.cub.2018.04.046**, original archived public article [PMC6331042](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6331042/).

The *Bacillus subtilis* WT produces two structural biofilm matrix components, EPS and TasA. The engineered Δeps mutant produces TasA alone; ΔtasA produces EPS alone. Neither forms the mature pellicle alone; together they can recover matrix production. Unlike *Pseudomonas* siderophores under severe iron scarcity, some **mixed ratios beat WT** in mature biofilm CFU productivity.

Source Figure 3: a cubic fit to pellicle productivity by Δeps mixing frequency **F(3,68)=54.9**, printed **R²=0.695**, **p<0.0001**. A separate negative frequency-dependence fit to the specialist relative fitness: **F(3,46)=94.7**, printed **R²=0.852**, **p<0.0001**. The productivity optimum is around **30% Δeps (TasA producer)**; competition over a short pellicle cycle yields approximate 20–30% Δeps coexistence. These **F tests are for dependence on specialist mixing ratio** and do not numerically measure the absolute WT-vs-mixture benefit. Do not report them as WT contrast effect sizes or count cubic fit degrees of freedom as independent evolution experiments.

Source Figure 4 independently includes **Arabidopsis root** CFU per millimetre, n=6 for WT and mix, p<0.04 for WT-vs-mix productivity. The three 2018 figure-level validations are **one programme** in two assay environments (pellicle/root), not independent architectural origins. A lower-growth short-term co-culture n would not calibrate BITA's historical fitness landscape.

A numerical consistency observation from the reported F statistics: converting F with three regression terms and residual degrees of freedom gives *raw* R² of approximately **0.70778** and **0.86065**, while the *adjusted* conventional R² equals approximately **0.69489** and **0.85156**. Thus the source captions' reported numbers **match adjusted R²** within rounding. Do not treat R²=0.695/0.852 as raw unadjusted explained variance without this qualification. These models are not physiological cost-curve calibrations. In the paper's individual-based **simulation**, positive net architecture benefit appears under stronger simultaneous-production constraint **f>1.1** (given its simulation setup); that threshold is model-specific and not empirically estimated.

**Data-access caveat:** the Europe PMC original fullTextXML endpoint for PMC6331042 returned HTTP 500/timeout in the source audit, so we retain source-indexed original article text and public PubMed figure captions for these figure statistics; no fabricated raw CFU measurements or archived original-JATS SHA is reported. The published paper states relevant underlying measurements can be requested from authors; the original per-replicate Figure 3/4 CFU measurements have not been independently recovered in this work.

## 2. Same study family: evolutionary adaptation can dissolve specialist dependencies

Dragoš et al. (2018), *Nature Microbiology*, DOI **10.1038/s41564-018-0263-y**, publisher [official article](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41564-018-0263-y). This later experimental-evolution study follows the same engineered Δeps and ΔtasA system and reports specialist cooperation collapse as ΔtasA outcompetes Δeps. Critically, the single-defect genotypes can evolve *autonomous pellicle formation*, with Δeps modification of remaining TasA and different routes to ΔtasA EPS enhancement. These are later mutations changing ecological interactions, **not merely drifting mixing ratios of two immutable mutant types**.

The original Nature **Supplementary Dataset 1** is public at [official XLSX](https://static-content.springer.com/esm/art%3A10.1038%2Fs41564-018-0263-y/MediaObjects/41564_2018_263_MOESM3_ESM.xlsx), **86,904 bytes**, SHA-256 **ec916667dbd7a288246737979e3b0d2e4336acfcc8dd98d641339973361d9a43**. The source also contains a mixture-population genome mutation inventory and clone-level mutation tables.

From the author **unique for eps** sheet, the original TasA cysteine substitutions are:

| Directly evolved Δeps lineage | Mutated gene | Source change | Clones with source variant |
|---|---|---|---|
| e4 | tasA | **Tyr124Cys** | e4A, e4B, e4C |
| e6 | tasA | **Gly183Cys** | e6A, e6B, e6C |

These are **two source lineages and six descendant isolates**, not six independent substitution origins. The gene-level source matrix is not a complete proof of direct biochemical causality on its own; the published experimental work supplies that interpretation through functional recreation experiments.

The original **resequenced populations (mix)** tab has three mixed population identifiers (Mix1/3/4), two engineered backgrounds (Δeps, ΔtasA), and three observed transfer times (5, 14, 24). Thus **six background-by-population series / 18 assayed snapshots**, *not* eighteen independent evolved architectures. Among exact X-positive mutation calls, five background-by-population loci are detected at transfer 24 that were not recorded at transfer 5; three are detected at transfer 5 but not transfer 24. Detection changes are **not exact mutation origin, clearance, fixation or phenotype-transition dates**. Four genotype-specific loci (two in each background) are present in all nine assayed snapshots of their background; these should **never** be recoded as four ninefold convergent evolutionary origins. Common starting genotypes and assay-specific ascertainment are alternative explanations.

The other source sheets contain many mutations in selected isolates and populations. A variant row is a mutation annotation / presence call, not the number of independently evolved functional solutions. The Source Excel provides **no matched measured longitudinal biofilm productivity** for the mixture samples. Hence we do not recalculate the later cooperation collapse as a continuous phenotype trajectory.

## 3. BITA interpretation: three distinct filters

1. **Contemporaneous architecture benefit**: Can the whole mixture beat a WT generalist *in a given medium and assay*? Bacillus reports **yes** in pellicles and plant-root biofilms at suitable mixture ratios. Pseudomonas siderophore specialists under severe iron limitation report **no**. These are different organismal/public-good systems and effect currencies. The signs are not contradictory observations of one universal effect.
2. **Ecological coexistence**: A negative frequency-dependent stable mixture at a fixed genotype architecture is possible on a 48-hour pellicle or 24-hour siderophore assay. It is a short-term ecological equilibrium within an assumed genotype set.
3. **Long-term evolutionary retention**: Novel mutations create alternative paths toward independence/autonomy or altered expression, so the previous genotype mixture can fail to persist even if its fixed-genotype ecological equilibrium is restoring. The 2018 long-term Bacillus follow-up demonstrates this is a real evolutionary possibility. Temporal stability and ancestral cause are separate from the existence of a profitable composition today.

The proper generalization for SCH–BITA is **conditional benefit × accessible alternatives × evolutionary retention**, with an explicit possibility that a new phenotype can be maintained via biochemical compensation rather than evolving a new permanently specialized module. This is a falsifiable design principle, not a calibrated universal fitness formula.

### Independent source and statistic constraints

- The two Bacillus articles share experimental system and research programme. Do **not** count as two independent biological replications of an architectural origin.
- A figure-level F statistic is not the mean WT/mix effect. Simulated f>1.1, observed root p<0.04, and mutation-supplement marker presence are different data layers.
- Isolate IDs e4A/B/C are descendants of *one e4 population*. Identical X-present mutations detected in every mixture at transfer 5 are not nine independent evolutions.
- The real data do not identify a historical switch from the WT's shared trait optimum to a newly evolved split architecture or BITA's specific exponential startup cost **K(u)**; the original mutations follow **engineered initial specialist architectures**.
- No pooled cross-species effect or probability of origin is estimated. Fitting pooled models from Bacillus CFU, Pseudomonas normalized OD AUC, eLife fluorescence and yeast Fcy1 rescue would violate outcome and selection comparability.
