# Three-chapter contract v1 — BITA

## Chapter order

```text
Chapter 1 / SCH
where does a shared trait compromise settle?

Chapter 2 / BALANCE
why does the compromise regime persist, and what is its internal geometry?

Chapter 3 / BITA
when and how does differentiated architecture become favourable?
```

## BITA responsibility

BITA owns the **differentiated-architecture world**. Its primary outputs are:

- recoverable compromise loss / dimensional release;
- architecture cost and residual coupling;
- the architecture margin `Phi=R-K` and its critical surface;
- direct tests of whether a retained axis moves toward a SCH reference under an added functional axis;
- mechanism identification once multiple axes exist.

BITA does not own the internal persistence/topology of the region `L>0, Phi<0`; that is Chapter 2 / BALANCE.

## Input from Chapter 2

BALANCE supplies the domain endpoint and, when identified, the critical environmental context where

```text
Phi(e)=0.
```

BITA asks what differentiated architecture exists beyond that boundary, how much of the shared compromise it actually releases, and through which ecological pathway the fitness advantage is generated.

## Back-reference to Chapter 1

When SCH supplies a state-specific reference such as `z_P*`, BITA may define dimensional release relative to that frozen reference. A pure-function reference must not be substituted unless SCH independently identified it.

## Claim ceiling

The chapter order is a conceptual decomposition of an architecture problem. It is not evidence that any lineage historically moved from Chapter 1 through Chapter 2 to Chapter 3 without independent historical data.
