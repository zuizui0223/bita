# BITA causal falsifiers v1

BITA is not supported merely because two traits exist or because one manipulation improves fitness. A causal differentiation claim must survive the following falsifiers.

## F0 — SCH reference invalid or mismatched

Reject the release test if the inherited SCH reference comes from a different context/scale or if a state-specific optimum is silently relabelled as a pure-function optimum.

## F1 — second axis does not carry the target function

If changing `y` does not alter the targeted functional outcome beyond the preregistered meaningful threshold, preferential loading is not established.

## F2 — cross-loading is too large

If `y` materially harms or changes the function meant to remain primarily on `x`, the simple division-of-labour interpretation fails. This may indicate strong residual coupling rather than no biology, but the strong BITA loading claim must be downgraded.

## F3 — no dimensional release

If

```text
R_state = |x0*-z_ref| - |x1*-z_ref|
```

is not positive beyond the preregistered threshold, the proposed second axis has not released the original trait from its SCH compromise in that context.

## F4 — release without fitness consequence

Positive `R_state` alone is insufficient. If access to the second axis does not produce the registered total-fitness consequence, classify geometric release separately from adaptive architecture advantage.

## F5 — net gain without identifiable decomposition

A positive direct `Delta_W` does not identify `R` and `K` separately. If no independent release or cost lane exists, do not claim a measured architecture cost or a mechanistic `R-K` decomposition.

## F6 — mechanism channels fail

If crossed ecological interventions do not support the proposed antagonist-relief, pollinator-interference, or joint-channel allocation, retain the positive architecture outcome if independently supported but downgrade the mechanism claim.

## F7 — local interaction cost is substituted for K

Any analysis that treats `kappa_delta`, a local interaction curvature, or a within-surface optimum difference as structural architecture cost `K` fails the registered cost semantics.

## F8 — historical claim exceeds contemporary evidence

Even a fully positive contemporary BITA experiment does not establish that the modeled conflict caused the historical origin of the differentiated traits.

## Interpretation of a failure

A failed BITA test can mean that the second axis is nonfunctional, strongly cross-loaded, unable to release the shared trait, too costly, or simply irrelevant in that environment. It does not automatically imply BALANCE or any PAYOFF game state without their own direct evidence.
