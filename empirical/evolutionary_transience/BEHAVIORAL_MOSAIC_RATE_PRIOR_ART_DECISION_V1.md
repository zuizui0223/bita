# Prior-art exclusion: behavioral buffering / drive as the next BITA headline

Date: 2026-10-08
Status: **NO PRIMARY NOVELTY CLAIM ON GENERAL MOSAIC RATE REDISTRIBUTION**
Branch: `analysis/ecological-stability-evolutionary-transience-v1`

After corrected Gate A failed, an alternative was considered:
"Acquiring clinging/robbing behavior reduces bill evolution but speeds
hallux/claw evolution in the same lineage."

This can be a *taxon-specific empirical prediction*, but **not** a newly
discovered general evolutionary principle.

## Close prior art

- Muñoz MM (2022), *The Bogert effect, a factor in evolution*,
  Evolution 76:49–66, doi:10.1111/evo.14388.
  Explicitly describes behavior simultaneously buffering some traits against
  selection and exposing other traits to morphological selection; an anole
  lizard case is provided.
- Colwell RK et al. (2023), *Repeated Evolution of Unorthodox Feeding Styles
  Drives a Negative Correlation between Foot Size and Bill Length in
  Hummingbirds*, American Naturalist 202:699–720, doi:10.1086/726036.
  Already documents replicated associations among clinging, shorter bills
  and longer hallux claws; ancestral paths and persistence are discussed.
- Barreto E et al. (2023), *Morphology and niche evolution influence hummingbird
  speciation rates*, Proc R Soc B, doi:10.1098/rspb.2022.1793.
  Already estimates trait and niche evolutionary rates and relationships to
  hummingbird speciation.

## Decision

- Do not relabel a known behavioral-inertia/behavioral-drive relationship
  as an original BITA theoretical discovery.
- Do not treat Colwell's association as proof of time-ordered evolutionary
  causality of behavioral changes on morphology.
- A future *independent* time-ordered rate-shift analysis could be useful
  as a new empirical case, but requires pre-outcome model comparisons,
  rate/historical-state uncertainty, and a compelling differentiator from
  Muñoz's existing conceptual framework.
- The failed clinger diversification Gate A cannot be rescued by replacing
  the outcome with claw rate in the same pre-registered analysis.

All main-paper claims remain unchanged. The status of the putative
ecological-persistence / evolutionary-transience cross-scale paradox remains
**not established**.
