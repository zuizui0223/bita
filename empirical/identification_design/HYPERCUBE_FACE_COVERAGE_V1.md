# A–D–G–P hypercube face coverage — v1

## Result

The target identification experiment has four conceptual axes:

```text
A = attraction
D = flower-associated antagonist-reducing defence
G = antagonist access/state
P = pollinator access/state
```

The current evidence does not contain the full strict `A × D × G × P` design. It does, however, contain **complementary lower-dimensional faces of that design in different studies**.

### Trait face

**Kessler et al. 2008** provides the strongest direct `A × D`-like floral trait factorial: benzylacetone × nicotine. Its main limitations are the systemic scope of nicotine manipulation and the absence of crossed consumer interventions.

### Attraction–consumer three-factor bridge

**Theis & Adler 2012** manipulated floral fragrance and crossed it with repeated beetle removal; supplemental hand pollination was then applied within each fragrance × beetle combination. This is an `A × G × P_supplementation` reproductive backbone. It lacks an independent D axis, and supplemental hand pollination is not a pollinator-access toggle.

### Defence–consumer three-factor bridge

**Santangelo et al. 2019** factorially crossed inherited HCN defence, herbivore suppression and hand pollination while measuring floral reproductive traits. This provides a `D × G × P_supplementation` backbone with observed A-like floral traits. Its D axis is whole-plant rather than flower-specific, A is not independently manipulated, and hand pollination is not a pollinator-access toggle.

### Consumer face with observed trait coordinates

**Egan et al. 2021** provides a strong herbivory × pollination consumer factorial while attraction- and defence-related traits are observed rather than independently crossed; several defence traits are leaf-derived.

### Observed trait × randomized context face

**Soper Gorden & Adler 2018** combines observational floral A/D coordinates with randomized robbing, florivory and pollination-intensity treatments. This reaches randomized context modification, not selective G/P access.

### Selective-D mechanism face

**Sun & Huang 2015** provides a physically interpretable flower-associated D manipulation that changes seed predation without a detected visitation effect, but lacks an independent A manipulation and separate consumer toggles.

## Synthesis

The design-fragmentation result is therefore stronger than `0/17 full identification`:

> **The literature already contains an A×D trait face, an A×G×pollination-supplementation face, a D×G×pollination-supplementation face, a G×P consumer face with measured traits, and a selective-D mechanism. These faces occur in different systems and do not yet close into one valid A×D×G×P experiment.**

This is exactly the empirical pattern predicted by an identification perspective: relevant biology and experimental competence are present, but the information needed for mechanism allocation is distributed across studies.

## Practical implication

The next experiment need not invent all four axes from scratch. Two especially direct routes are now visible:

1. **extend Theis-like A×G×pollination designs** by adding a distinct flower-associated D and a true selective pollinator-access/baseline treatment;
2. **extend Santangelo/Egan-like consumer designs** by adding independently manipulable, flower-specific A and D coordinates.

Kessler-like trait factorials offer the complementary route: retain A×D and add selective G/P interventions.

## Boundary

“Face coverage” is a structural comparison, not a claim that the treatments are equivalent. In particular:

- supplemental hand pollination is not pollinator absence/presence;
- manual beetle removal is not automatically perfect antagonist exclusion;
- whole-plant HCN is not a strict flower-specific D coordinate;
- observed A/D traits are not experimentally crossed A/D factors.

Accordingly, no lower-dimensional face is relabelled as full channel identification.
