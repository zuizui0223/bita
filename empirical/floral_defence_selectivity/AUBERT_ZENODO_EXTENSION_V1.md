# Aubert / EPHI all-Ecuador access-barrier extension v1

## Role

This is a second independent community/network-scale test of the BITA access-routing hypothesis.

It is **not** an exact replication of Aubert et al. 2026. The source paper analysed three Ecuador transects with its own trait sources and mixed-effects model. BITA uses the public EPHI Zenodo mirror across all 18 Ecuador sites and a deliberately simpler pair-site analysis.

Source paper: Aubert et al. 2026, *Oikos*, DOI 10.1002/oik.11552.

Public EPHI mirror: Zenodo DOI 10.5281/zenodo.14185547.

## Frozen trait rule

The source trait-matching logic is retained:

\[
M=\log(T/B)
\]

where:

- \(T\) = flower tube length;
- \(B\) = bird bill / culmen length;
- positive \(M\) means flower tube is longer than the bill.

BITA barrier state:

~~~text
TRAIT_BARRIER = T > B
~~~

Mirror units were audited before analysis:

~~~text
plant Tubelength = cm
bird culmen_length = mm
culmen converted to cm before matching
~~~

## Analysis grain

Resolved interaction rows are first joined to camera/site plant identity and bird/plant trait tables.

The inferential unit is then:

~~~text
bird species × plant species × site
~~~

with robbery rate calculated from resolved `piercing=yes/no` interactions.

Thus individual camera interactions are not treated as independent replicates.

## Coverage

~~~text
resolved target interactions: 21,114
trait-matched interactions:   20,572
pair-site units:               1,378
Ecuador sites represented:       18

hummingbird pair-sites:        1,377
flowerpiercer pair-sites:          1
~~~

The EPHI mirror provides almost no trait-matched flowerpiercer replication under the current join; the BITA extension is therefore effectively a hummingbird result and does not claim a flowerpiercer comparison.

## Result 1 — binary access barrier strongly separates robbery rate

~~~text
barrier pair-sites:    888
accessible pair-sites: 490

mean robbery rate:
  T > B barrier       = 0.30698
  T <= B accessible   = 0.08139

difference            = +0.22560
pair-site permutation p = 0.0001
~~~

Both medians are zero because robbery is zero-inflated; the mean contrast reflects a minority of pair-sites with substantial robbery.

## Result 2 — continuous mismatch tracks robbery

Across 1,378 pair-site units:

~~~text
Spearman rho[ log(T/B), robbery rate ] = 0.41826
permutation p = 0.0001
~~~

Thus increasing positive mismatch — flowers becoming longer relative to bird bills — is associated with higher nectar-robbing rates.

## Result 3 — the direction recurs across sites

Seventeen of the 18 Ecuador sites contain both barrier and accessible pair-sites and can be compared within site.

~~~text
eligible sites: 17
sites with barrier > accessible robbery: 15

mean within-site difference:   +0.14399
median within-site difference: +0.13132

two-sided sign-test p:          0.00235
site-stratified permutation p: 0.0001
~~~

This removes the concern that the pooled pair-site result is generated only by among-site composition.

## Result 4 — sparse pair-sites do not drive the barrier signal

The pair-site analysis was repeated after requiring at least 2 or at least 5 resolved interactions per bird × plant × site unit.

~~~text
minimum 1 interaction:
  n = 1,378
  barrier mean = 0.30698
  accessible mean = 0.08139
  difference = +0.22560
  mismatch rho = 0.41826
  both permutation p = 0.0001

minimum 2 interactions:
  n = 1,059
  barrier mean = 0.32485
  accessible mean = 0.07279
  difference = +0.25206
  mismatch rho = 0.47033
  both permutation p = 0.0001

minimum 5 interactions:
  n = 702
  barrier mean = 0.31999
  accessible mean = 0.05562
  difference = +0.26437
  mismatch rho = 0.50509
  both permutation p = 0.0001
~~~

Thus the access-barrier association is not generated by the large number of pair-site units represented by only one interaction. The direction and effect separation persist, and slightly strengthen descriptively, in more repeatedly observed pair-sites.

## Ecological interpretation

The result independently reproduces the routing prediction in a different pollinator system from Sakhalkar:

> **When flower tubes exceed the legitimate visitor's access morphology, nectar robbing becomes more common.**

Together, the two community-scale datasets show the same general access-routing pattern in different faunas:

~~~text
Sakhalkar 2023 — Afrotropical insect visitors
    longer tubes -> relatively more robbing than thieving

EPHI/Aubert extension — Ecuadorian hummingbird interactions
    tube > bill barrier -> higher robbery rate
    increasing log(T/B) -> higher robbery rate
~~~

## Claim boundary

Supported:

- strong association between access mismatch/barrier and interaction route;
- repeated direction across Ecuador sites;
- cross-dataset recurrence of access-routing logic.

Not supported:

- causal floral-defence effect;
- exact numerical replication of Aubert et al. 2026;
- a flowerpiercer-versus-hummingbird moderator test from this mirror;
- equivalence between the Sakhalkar and Aubert response scales;
- a universal effect size for access mismatch.
