# D-side route-level macro readout v1

## Why this lane exists

The strict matched-D analysis asks the strongest same-system selectivity question, but its direct Stage-2 subset is small.

The legacy BITA route ledger contains a broader D-side evidence base. This readout uses that base at its correct grain:

~~~text
56 total directional route records
17 unique D -> antagonism study programs after DOI-level deduplication
10 of those programs also contain a D -> pollination route
~~~

The Takeda 2021 slippery-perianth experiment is deduplicated across two historical cluster labels. See D_SIDE_ROUTE_DEDUPLICATION_AUDIT_V1.md.

## Result 1 — floral antagonist-reducing traits span multiple implementation classes

Among the 17 unique study programs:

~~~text
chemical      9
physical      7
reward/access 1
~~~

Antagonist-side source state:

~~~text
EFFECTIVE    16
UNRESOLVED    1
~~~

The unresolved program is *Impatiens capensis*, whose same-panel tannin–florivory direction conflicts with linked earlier evidence and remains zero-compatible.

### Interpretation

The D-side evidence base is not a single chemical-defence phenomenon. Antagonist reduction has been demonstrated across chemical, physical and reward/access implementations.

This is implementation breadth, not a prevalence estimate. D-role admission itself depends on antagonist-reduction evidence.

## Result 2 — pollinator consequences were measured in only part of the D corpus

Same-study D -> pollination follow-up exists for:

~~~text
10 / 17 programs = 58.8%
~~~

Coverage differs by broad implementation:

~~~text
chemical:      7 / 9
physical:      2 / 7
reward/access: 1 / 1
~~~

Chemical versus physical follow-up coverage:

~~~text
Fisher two-sided p = 0.1262
~~~

The sample is too small for a strong inferential claim, but the descriptive imbalance is important: the literature is substantially richer in pollinator-side follow-up for chemical than for physical defences.

### Consequence

The current richness of chemical dose/exposure switching evidence partly reflects what researchers measured, not necessarily a biological absence of physical selective windows.

This directly motivates targeted physical geometry/dose experiments rather than another broad chemical search.

## Result 3 — D -> pollination is heterogeneous rather than uniformly costly

Across the 10 programs with same-study D -> pollination information:

~~~text
CONTEXT_DEPENDENT  4
NULL_COMPATIBLE    3
IMPROVED           1
INTERFERENCE       1
UNRESOLVED         1
~~~

The four context-dependent programs are:

- *Polemonium* — dose/expression;
- *Asclepias* — cumulative exposure;
- *Nicotiana* — response stage;
- *Aconitum* — separated consumer dose thresholds.

The three null-compatible programs are *Ipomopsis*, *Pedicularis*, and *Catalpa*.

*Thunia* provides the improved/routing state. The fixed interference state is high-gelsemine *Gelsemium*. *Impatiens* remains unresolved.

### Ecological result

Within the source-adjudicated D-side corpus, the dominant pattern is not one fixed pollinator penalty.

Instead the data contain:

~~~text
state dependence + low-cost states + routing improvement + interference
~~~

This is the broader macro-level counterpart of the strict matched-D result.

It supports the effective-exposure interpretation:

> the ecological consequence of floral defence depends on consumer, exposure, access route and response stage rather than being a fixed property of the defence label.

## Result 4 — why this is stronger than the strict n=3 table but weaker than prevalence inference

The strict matched-D table has only three direction-supported Stage-2 systems because it requires unusually strong same-D and direct pollinator evidence.

The route-level lane is broader:

~~~text
17 unique D study programs
10 same-study pollinator follow-ups
~~~

but it deliberately relaxes the requirement that every program provide a directly comparable Stage-2 endpoint.

Therefore:

- use the route-level lane to establish breadth and state heterogeneity;
- use the matched-D lane to establish selectivity with stricter same-system interpretation;
- use the conditionality lane to establish within-system switching;
- use Sakhalkar to establish community-scale access routing.

Do not pool these layers into one pseudo-meta-analytic effect size.

    17 matched systems

## New high-value empirical gap

The route-level audit reveals a concrete design imbalance:

> **physical floral defences are common in the antagonist literature but rarely receive the same pollinator-side follow-up available for chemical defences.**

The next best empirical additions are therefore not generic new D studies. They are physical access/geometry manipulations that measure:

1. focal antagonist response;
2. legitimate pollinator response;
3. multiple barrier intensities or geometries;
4. bypass behaviour.

Those data would test the same effective-exposure theory while directly reducing the current chemical/physical follow-up imbalance.

## Claim boundary

None of the counts in this readout estimates natural prevalence.

The study corpus was assembled for mechanism evidence and is enriched for informative D systems.

The defensible macro claim is about recurrence, breadth, heterogeneity and measurement structure inside the audited evidence base.
