# Prior macro-synthesis positioning for the refocused BITA

## Purpose

This note protects the floral-defence selectivity refocus from rediscovering established macro-ecological results.

BITA's new question is deliberately narrower than "do herbivores affect pollination?" and broader than "do floral scents attract obligate visitors and repel facultative visitors?"

## Existing macro results that BITA must treat as prior art

### Junker & Blüthgen 2010 — floral scent visitor filtering

The Annals of Botany meta-analysis synthesized 18 published studies and 425 observations of animal responses to floral scent bouquets or floral scent compounds.

Its key result was that obligate flower visitors, often pollinators, were on average attracted, whereas facultative visitors, often antagonists, were on average repelled.

Therefore BITA must **not** claim novelty for the generic proposition:

```text
floral chemical cues can differentially affect pollinators and antagonists
```

or for a simple obligate-versus-facultative visitor filter in floral scent alone.

Source: Junker RR, Blüthgen N. 2010. Annals of Botany 105:777–782. DOI `10.1093/aob/mcq045`.

### Moreira et al. 2019 and the recent updated herbivory meta-analysis

These syntheses ask how herbivory or simulated damage changes floral traits, pollinator attraction, and reproductive output.

The recent update approximately doubled the earlier evidence base to 171 studies and 1348 observations and again found context-dependent negative effects of herbivory on floral traits, pollinator attraction, and reproduction.

Therefore BITA must **not** claim novelty for:

```text
herbivory can alter pollinator attraction
herbivore effects on pollination are context-dependent
```

Those analyses treat herbivory/damage as the exposure. Refocused BITA instead treats a flower-associated defence/access trait as the focal exposure.

### Caruso et al. 2019 — agents of selection on floral traits

This meta-analysis compares pollinator-mediated selection with selection from other biotic and abiotic agents.

Therefore BITA must **not** claim novelty for the broad statement that several ecological agents jointly shape floral traits.

## The remaining gap

The refocused BITA asks a different paired-trait question:

> Given one flower-associated antagonist-reducing trait or access architecture, when does that same D suppress antagonists while preserving legitimate pollination?

The required data structure is:

```text
same biological system
x same focal D
x antagonist response
x legitimate-pollinator / pollination-function response
```

The main explanatory variable is not consumer label alone and not chemical-versus-physical defence. It is whether antagonist and pollinator encounter the focal D in separated, overlapping, bypassed, or state-dependent effective domains.

This makes the intended contribution:

```text
prior art:
    visitor guilds can respond differently
    herbivory can alter pollination
    several agents select on floral traits

BITA:
    what ecological architecture predicts whether
    an antagonist-reducing floral trait is selective
    rather than pollinator-costly?
```

## Consequence for manuscript claims

A publishable positive result must show more than a mean guild difference.

The primary result should compare explicit competing explanations:

1. effective-domain relation;
2. chemical versus physical / access modality;
3. consumer guild identity;
4. study/design class where sample size permits.

The paired same-D structure is essential because unmatched antagonist and pollinator studies can reproduce a guild difference without showing that one defence trait selectively filters the two functions.

## Consequence for corpus construction

Historical meta-analyses may be used for:

- prior-art positioning;
- source discovery;
- external validation;
- compatible quantitative sub-lanes when primary-study provenance can be reconstructed.

They do not replace the matched-D corpus and their observations are not added as independent BITA clusters unless the primary biological unit passes the same matched-system gate.
