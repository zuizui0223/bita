# Polemonium viscosum 2011 dose-selectivity audit v1

## Source

Galen C, Kaczorowski R, Todd SL, Geib J, Raguso RA. 2011. **Dosage-dependent impacts of a floral volatile compound on pollinators, larcenists, and the potential for floral evolution in the alpine skypilot Polemonium viscosum.** *The American Naturalist* 177:258–272. DOI `10.1086/657993`.

Focal floral defence: 2-phenylethanol (2PE), a floral volatile whose natural emission spans more than two orders of magnitude.

## Matched biological result

The same 2PE axis affects both ant larcenists and bumblebee pollinators, but its state changes with dose.

The primary source reports that:

- naturally higher 2PE emitters escape ant damage;
- damaging ants are killed by sufficiently high 2PE exposure and avoid high emitters;
- high 2PE reduces bumblebee visitation and pollination;
- at moderate 2PE emission, more nectar remains in flowers without a detected pollination cost.

Thus one chemical mechanism moves between two states:

```text
moderate 2PE: antagonist/resource protection + no detected pollination cost
high 2PE:     antagonist deterrence + pollinator interference
```

## Mechanism-first interpretation

This is a critical counterbalance to guarded-defence examples. Selectivity is **not a fixed property of a defence mechanism**. It can collapse as expression intensity increases.

The study therefore predicts a dose-dependent switching surface:

```text
selective/guarded region --increasing dose--> non-selective/interference region
```

This is exactly the kind of conditional transition expected under the mechanism-first framing: the same constituent pathway can support complementarity at one expression level and substitution at another without changing trait identity.

## Quantitative status

The current source-audited ledger preserves the dose-specific state directions, but a common uncertainty-bearing matched effect for ant protection and pollinator service has not yet been reconstructed from the published figures/tables. The source's qualitative dose switch is strong and primary-source verified; it is not converted into an invented meta-analytic contrast.

## Inference boundary

This study does not estimate direct `A x D`, `W_AD`, or `kappa`. The moderate/high contrast should be used to test the universality of a switching rule, not to classify 2PE as universally selective or universally costly.
