# Mechanism-first universality readout v5

## Scientific frame

This project proceeds **Mechanism -> predicted Pattern -> replication / universality test**.

The empirical target is not a universal grand mean. The target is whether the mechanism classes and switching rules fixed in Part I recur independently across taxa, signal modalities, defence implementations, consumer guilds, attack routes and response stages.

## U2 — cross-modal shared tracking is now a replicated pattern

The recurrence of `A -> antagonism` is not restricted to scent.

### Visual same-axis systems

- **Dalechampia scandens**: +1 SD bract area changes source-predicted pollinator patch visitation from 83.1% to 94.4% while seed predation rises from about 0.2% to 2.5%.
- **Hibiscus moscheutos**: direct petal-size manipulation shows petals are required visual cues for legitimate pollinators, while adult pollen predators are denser on intact/larger-petal flowers. The antagonist response does not propagate to later seed predation, showing response-stage dependence.
- **Ipomopsis aggregata**: direct manipulation of floral display size increases hummingbird discovery / total flower visits and also increases pre-dispersal seed predation (`P<0.001`).
- **Raphanus** and **Silene** independently extend the pattern to colour and combined colour+scent axes.

### U2 interpretation

> Floral apparency that improves discoverability to mutualists can also be available to antagonists across olfactory and visual modalities.

A universal numeric visual effect is still not identified because the available manipulated visual systems rarely report compatible uncertainty-bearing effect structures.

## U5/U6 — physical selectivity is independently replicated

The chemical-only explanation is now rejected.

### Physical selective implementations

1. **Pedicularis rex — attack-mode selectivity**  
   Water-filled bracts strongly reduce seed predation while nectar-robber and pollinator visitation remain null-compatible; robbers can bypass the barrier by piercing above it.

2. **Thunia alba — functional-mode selectivity**  
   A spur-enclosing bract suppresses robbery, leaves arrival rate approximately unchanged and shifts the same Bombus toward legitimate pollination, increasing pollen transfer and fruit set.

3. **Codonopsis lanceolata — spatial/access geometry**  
   Slippery wax occupies ant approach surfaces while a non-slippery basal pollinator foothold remains usable. Large hornets can bridge the defended surface; small ants cannot unless provided an artificial bridge.

4. **Chrysothemis friedrichsthaliana — temporal/developmental selectivity**  
   A water calyx protects pre-anthesis buds from alucitid moth oviposition. Draining more than doubles infestation/sterilization odds (`OR=2.18`, 95% CI `1.21–3.92`). Legitimate hummingbird pollination occurs after anthesis, separating antagonist and pollinator access in developmental time.

### Physical boundary systems

- **Bejaria resinosa**: sticky floral surfaces strongly reduce florivory but can exclude mutualistic insects as well; physical defence is therefore not inherently selective.
- **Salvia miltiorrhiza**: calyx shortening changes floral longevity and fitness but neither robber nor pollinator visitation; robbers bite the exposed corolla and bypass the calyx. A conspicuous structure is not an effective focal anti-robber D merely because it looks protective.

### U5/U6 interpretation

The recurrent abstract rule is now best expressed as **effective-domain separation**:

```text
selective / guarded state
    occurs while antagonist and pollinator effective defence domains remain separable

non-selective / failed state
    occurs when those domains overlap or when the antagonist bypasses the defence domain
```

Chemical systems separate domains by physiological threshold and cumulative exposure; physical systems separate them by attack mode, body size, functional route, spatial contact zone or developmental timing.

## Direct A x D / kappa — final targeted design result

Repeated targeted searches continue to recover studies that are biologically close but fail the strict identification gate:

- factorial pollinator x herbivore manipulations (`Helleborus`, `Fragaria`) manipulate ecological agents rather than A and D traits;
- scent-decomposition and dual-function studies (`Cucurbita`, `Petunia`, `Nicotiana`) do not independently vary two validated A and D axes on one shared fitness outcome;
- phenotypic-selection studies can estimate agent-mediated selection on attractive and defensive traits, but not the explicit trait-by-trait interaction required for `W_AD`;
- candidate protective structures that fail the flower-specific D gate or antagonist-efficacy gate are not promoted to fill the matrix.

The empirical gap is therefore increasingly specific:

> the field rarely performs a crossed manipulation of a validated floral attraction trait and a separately validated flower-specific defence trait, followed by an explicit A:D interaction test on a common reproductive/fitness outcome.

U7 and U8 should remain **unidentified field-design gaps**, not be inferred as zero.

## Current strongest synthesis

> Attraction signals repeatedly expose flowers to both mutualists and antagonists across olfactory and visual modalities. Flower-specific defence repeatedly reduces antagonist access through chemical and physical mechanisms. Whether pollination is preserved depends on whether the antagonist and pollinator effective interaction domains remain separable. Chemical dose and cumulative exposure, and physical attack mode, space, body size, behaviour and developmental timing, all instantiate the same switching architecture. Boundary systems behave as predicted when domains overlap or when antagonists bypass the putative defence. The recurrent empirical object is therefore a cross-modal domain-separation/selectivity-window rule, not one universal sign of attraction–defence association.

## What remains unsupported

- a universal positive or negative `W_AD`;
- a universal numeric threshold ratio;
- a common meta-analytic effect combining visitation, handling, predation, oviposition and fitness;
- `kappa = 0`;
- prevalence claims from route counts;
- a second strict direct A x D cluster.

## Stop-rule update

The broad pattern-class search is saturated. U2, U5 and U6 now have cross-modal replication with explicit counterexamples/boundaries. Further evidence work should continue **only** for:

1. a genuinely new strict direct A x D trait design;
2. a strict direct joint-cost estimate;
3. a scale-compatible matched outcome lane capable of formal moderator inference.

Absent one of those three, additional broad or mechanism-class searching is no longer expected to change the central Pattern conclusion.
