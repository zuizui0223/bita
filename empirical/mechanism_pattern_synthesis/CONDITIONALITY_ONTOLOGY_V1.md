# Conditionality ontology and Gate D adjudication v1

## Decision

**Gate D — sign-switch / conditionality analysis: PASS for the current source-adjudicated evidence architecture.**

The pass is based on an explicit theory-facing map, not on a pooled universal moderator coefficient. `SIGN_SWITCH_LEDGER_V1.csv` contains 11 independent study clusters with within-study or within-system contrasts that change the observed mechanism state across dose, expression, reward, exposure, consumer, response definition, or trait component. These contrasts remain dependent within study and are never counted as independent replications.

`COMPLETION_GATE_V1.md` allows conditionality to be **tested or mapped where the data permit**. A cross-study meta-regression is not defensible here because the registered contrasts mix visitation, preference, residence/consumption, physiological detection, reproductive components, antagonist entry/exploitation, and compound-specific consumer use. Forcing them onto one effect scale would violate the outcome-lane and independence guardrails.

## Compact moderator ontology

Each of the 11 registered sign-switch clusters is assigned once to a primary theory-facing class. The purpose is to reduce the original fine-grained axes without manufacturing quantitative comparability.

| Ontology class | Independent clusters | Registered examples | Theory-facing meaning |
|---|---:|---|---|
| **trait intensity / expression regime** | 3 | `Polemonium` expression level; `Asclepias` natural vs elevated dose; `Nicotiana` time-of-night emission | A mechanism can be absent, guarded, or interfering at different amounts or expression windows of the same trait axis. |
| **resource / exposure context** | 2 | `Gelsemium` sugar compensation; `Asclepias` short vs multi-day exposure | Reward value and experience can change whether a defensive trait is tolerated or avoided. |
| **consumer identity / functional role** | 2 | `Nicotiana` pollinator identity; `Aconitum` robber vs legitimate pollinator threshold | The same floral axis can have different response thresholds or dependencies in different consumer guilds/species. |
| **response definition / stage / reproductive scale** | 3 | `Nicotiana` visit number vs residence/removal; `Impatiens` two reproductive components; `Gelsemium` plant entry vs within-plant exploitation | Apparent sign/state depends on what stage of interaction or reproductive component is measured. |
| **compound identity / mechanism partition** | 1 | `Cucurbita` scent compounds partitioned among pollinator and herbivore responses | Different components of a floral signal blend can map to different consumers rather than one compound having one universal effect. |

Total: **11 independent study clusters across five theory-facing conditionality classes**.

These counts describe recurrence in the registered information-rich evidence set. They are **not prevalence estimates in nature** and are not used as weights or parameter estimates.

## What is quantitatively resolved inside studies

Where source-compatible quantitative contrasts exist, the project retains the source-level resolution rather than replacing it with ontology counts. Examples include:

- `Gelsemium`: reconstructed preference effects show the high-gelsemine disadvantage under equal sugar is removed when sugar reward compensates it;
- `Aconitum`: robber consumption is strongly reduced at substantially lower reported alkaloid concentrations than the source-reported sharp decline in legitimate pollinator visitation;
- `Impatiens`: the only strict direct `A x D` cluster has opposite point signs across two reproductive components, but both interaction intervals include zero;
- `Nicotiana` 2015: floral-scent dependence differs strongly between two pollinator species in the same experimental plant panel.

These are within-study/context results, not four independent estimates of one common moderator effect.

## Why no cross-study moderator test is promoted

A formal moderator synthesis would currently require at least one of the following invalid moves:

1. treating different biological outcomes as one response variable;
2. treating doses, time points, consumers, or reproductive components from one experiment as independent studies;
3. assigning numerical magnitudes to directional states that were only source-reported categorically;
4. selecting one outcome after seeing which direction is most favorable.

The registered evidence therefore supports a **mapped conditionality result**, not a cross-outcome grand moderator coefficient.

## Theory-facing result

The evidence rejects the empirical simplification that attraction tracking, antagonist relief, or pollinator interference has one context-free observed state. Conditionality recurs through at least five distinct biological routes: trait expression, resource/exposure context, consumer identity, response stage/scale, and compound identity.

This is precisely the empirical pattern the fixed theory is designed to organize: the local attraction–defence relationship can change because the relative expression of antagonist relief, pollinator interference, and associated costs changes across biological contexts. The map does **not** identify `rho`, `iota`, `kappa`, or `W_AD`.

## Gate D status

```text
registered sign-switch study clusters: 11
primary ontology classes:             5
cross-outcome grand moderator:        not estimated
reason:                               effect/outcome incompatibility and within-study dependence
Gate D:                               PASS_AS_CONDITIONALITY_MAP
```

A future literature expansion may support quantitative meta-regression inside one compatible outcome/mechanism lane. That would refine this gate; it is not required to replace the present defensible map with an invalid universal mean.
