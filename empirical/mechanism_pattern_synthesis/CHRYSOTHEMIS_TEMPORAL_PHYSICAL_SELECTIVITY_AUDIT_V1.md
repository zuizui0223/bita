# Chrysothemis temporal physical-selectivity audit v1

## Purpose

Test whether the selectivity-window principle has an independent **physical / temporal-stage** implementation outside the Takeda slippery-perianth program.

Primary source: Carlson JE, Harms KE. 2007. *The benefits of bathing buds: water calyces protect flowers from a microlepidopteran herbivore*. Biology Letters 3:405–407. DOI `10.1098/rsbl.2007.0095`.

## Focal flower-specific defence

`Chrysothemis friedrichsthaliana` has liquid-filled water calyces that submerge developing floral buds before anthesis.

The experiment directly manipulated the physical barrier by draining versus retaining/refilling calyx liquid.

### Antagonist response

Draining the calyx more than doubled the odds of alucitid larval infestation / floral sterilization:

```text
odds ratio drained vs water-filled = 2.18
95% CI = 1.21–3.92
F1,241 = 6.87
P = 0.009
```

The source attributes the difference primarily to lower oviposition in immersed buds rather than lower larval survival.

Generalist herbivores were unaffected (`P=0.88`), so the defence is already consumer/attack-mode selective rather than uniformly protective.

## Selectivity interpretation

The physical barrier is expressed during the **bud / pre-anthesis stage**, when the main antagonist oviposits. Legitimate hummingbird pollination occurs after the corolla opens. Thus the defence and pollination channels are separated primarily in time and developmental stage rather than by chemical sensitivity or contact geometry.

This provides an independent physical realization of the same abstract principle:

```text
antagonist access domain: pre-anthesis submerged bud
pollinator access domain: post-anthesis open corolla
```

The study does not manipulate pollinator access directly, so it is not a matched `D -> pollination` quantitative anchor equivalent to Catalpa/Pedicularis/Thunia. It is retained as an independent **temporal-stage physical selectivity** program.

## Boundary and falsification content

The water barrier does not deter every antagonist. Small nectar-thieving ants can bypass the calyx and enter the open corolla, and generalist herbivore damage was unaffected by the water treatment. Thus the defence is selective because of the antagonist's attack route and life stage, not because water is a universal floral barrier.

## Inference boundary

```text
pre-anthesis temporal separation != direct proof of zero pollinator cost
one physical temporal system != prevalence
water calyx defence != universal barrier against floral antagonists
ant bypass != failure of the focal moth-defence mechanism
```
