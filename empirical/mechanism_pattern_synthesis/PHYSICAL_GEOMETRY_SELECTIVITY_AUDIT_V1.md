# Physical geometry selectivity audit v1

## Purpose

Test whether the mechanism-first `selectivity window` recurs in a non-chemical defence where selectivity is generated by **spatial geometry**, not concentration.

## Codonopsis lanceolata / Fritillaria koidzumiana

Primary source: Takeda, Kadokawa & Kawakita 2021, *Annals of Botany* 127:231–239, DOI `10.1093/aob/mcaa168`.

### Focal physical defence

The perianths carry epicuticular wax crystals that make specific flower surfaces slippery to nectar-thieving ants. Field bridging experiments show that bypassing the slippery surface increases ant entry into flowers.

Source-reported flower-level context:

```text
Codonopsis lanceolata:
control flowers ever receiving ants ~10%
bridged flowers ever receiving ants ~28%

Fritillaria koidzumiana:
control flowers ever receiving ants ~5.1%
bridged flowers ever receiving ants ~45%
```

The source models repeated ant-presence records rather than these rounded at-least-once proportions, so these percentages remain descriptive and are not reverse-engineered into canonical uncertainty-bearing effects.

### Spatial selectivity in Codonopsis

The key mechanistic observation is that the slippery defence is **not distributed uniformly across the flower**.

- the abaxial surface and distal adaxial surface are slippery and densely wax-covered;
- the basal adaxial surface, where legitimate Vespa pollinators place their forelegs while collecting nectar and contacting the reproductive column, is not slippery and lacks dense wax crystals;
- the transition between slippery and non-slippery surfaces is anatomically distinct.

Therefore the physical defence creates an access geometry in which ant approach surfaces are defended while the pollinator foothold is preserved.

### Downstream pollinator consequence of antagonist exclusion

The source independently introduced ants inside Codonopsis flowers. Hornets encountering ants withdrew and left early; ant presence significantly shortened pollinator visit duration. Visitation frequency and downstream fruit/seed outcomes did not resolve into a simple corresponding effect.

This ant-introduction experiment is not relabelled as `D -> pollination`; it demonstrates why excluding ants can protect a pollinator-handling pathway.

## Mechanism-first interpretation

This system supplies a spatial implementation of interaction selectivity:

```text
ant access pathway:          slippery / defended
pollinator foothold pathway: non-slippery / preserved
```

The resulting selectivity is generated by **where** defence is expressed rather than only **how much** defence is expressed.

It therefore independently supports the higher-level switching principle already seen in chemical threshold systems:

> defence can suppress antagonist access while preserving pollinator function when antagonist and pollinator interaction paths occupy separable spatial or response domains.

### Fritillaria boundary

Fritillaria additionally illustrates that geometry can locally close the defence channel: hairy processes on the tepal margin/inner surface provide gripping structures, so ants can exploit alternative access features. This is retained as attack-route context rather than a second independent replication.
