# Rivest et al. 2024 pollen-defence universality audit v1

## Source

Rivest S, Lee ST, Cook D, Forrest JRK. 2024. **Consequences of pollen defense compounds for pollinators and antagonists in a pollen-rewarding plant.** *Ecology* 105:e4306. DOI `10.1002/ecy.4306`.

Study system: *Lupinus argenteus*. The flower is nectarless and pollen is the sole floral reward. Pollen contains quinolizidine alkaloids, so the candidate defence trait is unambiguously located in a floral reproductive/reward tissue.

## Why this source matters for the mechanism-first universality test

The study simultaneously measures a single floral-chemistry axis against multiple interaction partners:

- pollen thieves: thrips and pollen beetles;
- pollen-colonizing bacteria;
- legitimate bee pollinators;
- laboratory bacterial isolates exposed to thermopsine.

It therefore directly tests whether a defence-like floral chemistry has one generic effect or whether its ecological state depends on antagonist identity/tolerance and response stage.

## Source results

### Pollen thieves

No association was detected between pollen alkaloid concentration and either pollen-beetle abundance (`n=129`, `Z=-1.03`, `P=0.30`) or thrips abundance (`n=129`, `Z=0.012`, `P=0.99`).

Thus the candidate pollen defence does **not** show the expected pollen-thief deterrence route in this system.

### Pollen-associated bacteria

Field bacterial abundance was negatively associated with pollen alkaloid concentration (`n=129`, `Z=-3.02`, `P=0.0026`).

However, the direct laboratory thermopsine assay on three common bacterial morphotypes did not detect growth inhibition over naturally relevant tested concentrations (`n=60`, `Z=-1.08`, `P=0.28`).

The paper explicitly cautions that the ecological role of pollen-associated bacteria and the causal interpretation of the field association remain uncertain.

### Pollinators

Higher pollen alkaloid concentration was associated with **more bee visits** (`n=129`, `Z=2.14`, `P=0.032`). The same plants had shorter visitation bouts, leaving the total number of flowers visited per plant approximately unchanged. The authors therefore conclude that pollen alkaloids are unlikely to impose a strong negative pollination cost at the observed field concentrations.

## Adjudication against focal D

The organ gate passes: the chemistry occurs in pollen, a floral tissue/reward.

The **functional D gate does not yet pass strictly for a focal animal antagonist route**:

- pollen thieves are not reduced;
- bacterial abundance is negatively associated with alkaloids, but the common-isolate manipulation is null and bacterial harm to plant fitness is not established.

Accordingly this source must **not** be added to the route-ledger `D -> antagonism` count merely because the compounds are chemically defensive in other contexts.

## Universality value

This is a high-value **falsification/context case** for the interaction-selectivity hypothesis.

It shows that:

1. chemical identity or the label “defence compound” does not guarantee realized defence against every antagonist guild;
2. consumer/taxon tolerance can close an otherwise plausible defensive pathway;
3. response stage matters: a field association with microbial abundance need not reproduce as direct inhibition of common isolates;
4. pollinator responses can change in visit number and bout duration without a net reduction in flowers visited.

This therefore strengthens the higher-level prediction that ecological selectivity is generated by **consumer identity × exposure × response stage**, rather than by defence chemistry alone.

## Inference boundary

Do not treat a defensive chemical label as proof of focal `D`. Do not treat the negative field bacteria association as causal pathogen defence, and do not interpret changed bee bout structure as a direct estimate of `iota` or `W_AD`.
