# Anemone coronaria 2025 dual-role consumer context audit v1

## Source

Saabna N, Keasar T, Sapir Y. **The roles of florivory and herbivory in maintaining intra-population flower colour variation in Anemone coronaria.** *Plant Biology* 27:163–171. DOI `10.1111/plb.13744`.

The primary article is open access (PMCID `PMC11656279`).

## Pattern question

Can the same flower visitor occupy both mutualistic and antagonistic functional roles, and does floral damage necessarily suppress subsequent pollinator attraction?

This study is useful because it separates those questions within a colour-polymorphic floral system.

## Same-consumer dual role

Red *Anemone coronaria* flowers had more petal scratch damage than purple and white morphs. The scratches were attributed to glaphyrid beetles.

Glaphyrid beetles are also the major pollinators of the red morph. The source therefore identifies the same consumer guild as:

```text
mutualist role:    pollen transfer / pollination of red flowers
antagonist role:   petal scratching / florivory on red flowers
```

By contrast, bite damage attributed to caterpillars or grasshoppers was less frequent on red flowers than on white/purple flowers.

This adds a strong consumer-identity/functional-role Pattern: flower colour changes which antagonist damage type is associated with a morph, and one of those damage types is produced by the morph's principal pollinator guild.

## Experimental damage → pollinator response

The authors experimentally removed 0, 0.5, 1, 5, 10, or 30% of petal area and monitored visitors in field arrays.

Published result:

```text
343 putative pollinator visits
Hymenoptera: 253
Diptera:       88
Coleoptera:     2
petal-damage effect on visit number:
chi-square = 0.541
P = 0.461
```

Thus simulated petal damage over the tested 0–30% range did not reduce pollinator visitation in any colour morph.

This is an independent reminder that antagonist damage does not automatically imply a pollinator-suppression channel.

## Fitness context

Despite colour-dependent damage types and levels, red, purple and white morphs had similar maternal fitness in the study. The authors interpret this as potentially reflecting a balance among multiple mutualistic and antagonistic effects rather than one agent dominating all outcomes.

This is a Pattern observation, not an estimate of the focal mixed partial.

## Theory-facing mapping

Admitted:

```text
same consumer guild can switch between mutualistic and antagonistic functional roles
consumer identity changes damage pattern associated with floral colour
damage in the observed experimental range need not suppress pollinator visitation
multiple opposing biological contributions can yield similar total fitness among morphs
```

Not admitted:

```text
flower colour association = direct A x D
simulated damage = defence trait D
P=0.461 = iota = 0
glaphyrid scratch damage = W_AD

```

## Route-ledger boundary

This study is **context-only** in the current expansion.

The colour morph is a plausible attraction/display axis, but the paper does not provide the required same-unit direct pollinator-response contrast to colour needed for a new `A -> pollination` route record in this dataset. The damage manipulation is an antagonist-pressure treatment, not a floral defence trait `D`.

Accordingly, it does not increment the 18 route-ledger clusters.
