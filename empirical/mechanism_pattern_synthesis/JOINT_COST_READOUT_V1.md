# Direct attraction–defence joint-cost audit v1

## Question

The theory contains a direct joint-cost curvature term, `kappa`, separate from antagonist relief and pollinator interference. The empirical question is therefore narrow:

> Is there a source-verified study that directly measures an allocation, construction, physiological, or reproductive cost that depends jointly on a declared floral attraction axis `A` and a declared flower-specific defence/access axis `D`?

Trait covariance, genetic linkage, pleiotropy, or failure to detect a negative correlation do not by themselves identify this term.

## Current audited candidates

Three high-information sources have been checked because they explicitly combine attraction-associated floral traits and defence-associated chemistry.

### Theis et al. (2014), Cucurbitaceae

The paper explicitly tested an allocation-tradeoff prediction. Across varieties, neither leaf nor floral cucurbitacins correlated with any measured floral attractive or reward trait:

```text
|r| < 0.25
P > 0.05 for all comparisons
```

The authors therefore report no support for simple allocation trade-offs between attractive and defensive functions in this comparative panel.

This is useful negative evidence against a simple phenotypic tradeoff, but it is **not** a direct cost measurement. A zero or weak covariance can arise even with costs, and a covariance can arise from shared genetics or selection without a cost. This result cannot be translated into `kappa = 0`.

### Thosteman et al. (2024), *Arabis alpina*

A 28-population common garden quantified integration within and among floral scent, foliar volatiles, and foliar glucosinolates. Between-group integration was low:

```text
floral scent × foliar volatiles:    integration index 4.18
floral scent × foliar glucosinolates: integration index 4.24
```

The authors conclude that the chemical groups show little to no integration and can evolve largely independently. They also report no strong negative correlations among compound groups.

This is again informative about phenotypic/evolutionary integration, not a direct allocation cost. It is additionally cross-organ: the putative defensive axes are foliar rather than flower-specific. It therefore cannot identify the theory's floral `A x D` cost.

### Strauss, Irwin & Lambrix (2004), wild radish

This study links petal-colour polymorphism to petal and leaf glucosinolate architecture and discusses pleiotropy or tight genetic linkage as possible explanations for correlated floral/defence traits. It directly measures defensive chemistry in petals, which makes it closer in organ alignment than many defence studies.

However, no direct resource expenditure, construction cost, physiological cost, or `A x D` allocation term is measured. Genetic linkage and pleiotropy are alternative mechanisms to an allocation constraint, so this source remains joint-trait architecture rather than joint-cost evidence.

## Current evidence state

```text
direct measured A+D allocation/construction cost: 0 verified studies
explicit simple allocation-tradeoff test:           1 verified negative panel
low attraction/defence chemical integration:        1 verified cross-organ panel
joint floral signal/defence architecture:           1 verified non-cost panel
kappa identified:                                    no
kappa estimated as zero:                             no
```

The current result is therefore not "joint costs are absent." The result is:

> In the closest source-verified studies located so far, simple phenotypic or chemical trade-offs are weak or absent, but none directly measures the joint cost required to identify `kappa`.

## Consequence for the synthesis

This distinction is useful for the integrated paper. It prevents a common inferential shortcut in which negative trait covariance is labelled an allocation cost, while also allowing a genuine evidence-gap result if a saturated search continues to find no direct joint-cost study.

Completion Gate F remains **active, not passed**. It may eventually pass either by locating a direct cost study or by documenting search saturation and reporting `kappa` as empirically unidentified.
