# Physical selectivity replication and boundary audit v1

## Question

Does the selectivity-window principle recur among physical floral traits, or is it only a chemical-dose phenomenon?

## Replicated physical implementations

### 1. Pedicularis rex — attack-mode selectivity

Water-filled bracts strongly reduce seed predation while nectar-robber and legitimate-pollinator visitation are null-compatible. Robbers can bypass the barrier by piercing above the water.

### 2. Thunia alba — functional-mode routing

A spur-enclosing bract strongly suppresses nectar robbery while leaving arrival frequency approximately unchanged and increasing legitimate pollination / reproductive function. The same Bombus shifts behaviour when the bract is removed.

### 3. Codonopsis lanceolata — spatial/access geometry

Epicuticular wax is concentrated on antagonist approach surfaces while the basal inner pollinator foothold remains non-slippery. Large hornet pollinators can bridge the slippery zone; small ants cannot unless an artificial bridge is supplied.

### 4. Chrysothemis friedrichsthaliana — temporal/developmental geometry

The water calyx protects the pre-anthesis bud from alucitid oviposition (`OR drained vs filled = 2.18`, 95% CI `1.21–3.92`) while legitimate hummingbird pollination occurs after anthesis. Generalist herbivores and nectar-thieving ants are not blocked, showing attack-route specificity.

## Physical boundary / falsification systems

### Bejaria resinosa — broad sticky filter

Floral stickiness strongly reduces florivory, but the sticky surface can exclude mutualistic as well as antagonistic insects. The predicted stickiness x hummingbird-exclusion rescue interaction was not detected. Physical defence is therefore not intrinsically selective.

### Salvia miltiorrhiza — geometry that fails the antagonist gate

Calyx shortening directly manipulated a conspicuous physical floral structure, but neither nectar-robber nor pollinator visitation changed (`P=0.593` and `P=0.207`). Robbers simply bite the uncovered corolla region; the calyx does not constitute an effective focal anti-robber D in this system. The manipulation strongly altered floral longevity and fitness through other calyx functions, so fitness differences cannot be relabelled antagonist relief.

## Cross-system inference

The physical evidence rejects a simple category rule such as:

> physical defence is pollinator-safe.

Instead it supports a domain-separation rule:

> physical defence is selective only when antagonist access and pollinator access are mechanically, spatially, temporally, or functionally separable.

Selective cases (`Pedicularis`, `Thunia`, `Codonopsis`, `Chrysothemis`) and boundary cases (`Bejaria`, `Salvia`) are both predicted by this rule.
