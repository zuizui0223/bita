# Catalpa speciosa 1982 floral-nectar defence audit v1

## Source

Stephenson AG. **Iridoid glycosides in the nectar of Catalpa speciosa are unpalatable to nectar thieves.** *Journal of Chemical Ecology* 8:1025–1034. DOI `10.1007/BF00987883`.

The primary abstract is indexed in PubMed, and an author-uploaded copy of the primary article exposes the experimental tables used below.

## Why this is a high-value floral-D system

The focal defence is expressed directly in floral nectar:

```text
D = catalpa iridoid glycosides in floral nectar
experimental chemical treatment = sucrose + 0.4% catalpol + 0.4% catalposide
```

The study asks whether the same nectar chemistry deters potential nectar thieves while remaining palatable to legitimate pollinators.

This is therefore an unusually clean same-study consumer comparison for a flower-specific chemical defence axis.

## Potential nectar thieves

### Ants

Potential thief ants were offered natural nectar, matched sucrose solution, and sucrose solution containing catalpa iridoids.

Source Table 2:

```text
nectar:                         n=58,  25 ± 41 s drinking
matched sucrose:                n=38, 118 ± 73 s
sucrose + catalpa iridoids:     n=52,  33 ± 49 s

nectar vs sucrose:              P < 0.001
sucrose vs sucrose+iridoids:    P < 0.001
nectar vs sucrose+iridoids:     P > 0.10
```

Ants consuming nectar or iridoid-containing sucrose also displayed abnormal behaviour, whereas ants consuming pure sucrose did not.

### Skipper butterfly

Potential nectar-thief *Poanes hobomok* similarly consumed much less nectar or iridoid-containing sucrose than pure sucrose.

Source Table 3:

```text
nectar:                         n=14, 3.2 ± 1.4 µl
matched sucrose:                n=21, 9.5 ± 6.3 µl
sucrose + catalpa iridoids:     n=22, 3.7 ± 3.2 µl

nectar vs sucrose:              P < 0.001
sucrose vs sucrose+iridoids:    P < 0.001
nectar vs sucrose+iridoids:     not resolved (0.30 < P < 0.40)
```

Behavioural abnormalities also occurred after nectar or iridoid-containing sucrose.

Pattern state:

```text
D -> nectar-thief antagonism: strong deterrence / reduced palatability
```

## Legitimate pollinator response

Legitimate daytime pollinators — *Bombus bimaculatus*, *B. fervidus*, *B. impatiens*, *B. vagans*, and *Xylocopa* sp. — were tested on an artificial flower board with the same three reward classes.

Source Table 4:

```text
natural nectar:                 50 wells, 19.3 ± 2.4 µl consumed
matched sucrose:                54 wells, 19.7 ± 1.4 µl
sucrose + catalpa iridoids:     20 wells, 19.5 ± 1.4 µl
Kruskal-Wallis H = 0.43, df=2, P > 0.20
```

No abnormal behaviour was observed in the legitimate pollinators after consuming any reward.

Pattern state:

```text
D -> pollinator reward use: no detected cost in the tested pollinator guild
```

This is a guarded state, not a claim that all pollinators universally tolerate defensive nectar chemistry.

## Independence value

This study predates and is biologically independent of the current Aconitum alkaloid cluster. The two systems also differ in the degree of pollinator sensitivity:

```text
Catalpa iridoids: legitimate pollinator consumption unresolved from controls
Aconitum alkaloids: natural field variation correlates negatively with pollinator visitation, while robbers are deterred at low concentrations
```

That contrast strengthens the central Pattern that floral chemical defence does not have one context-free pollinator consequence.
