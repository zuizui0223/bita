# Direct A+D joint-cost (`kappa`) search saturation receipt v1

## Decision

**Gate F — direct joint-cost evidence state: PASS as a documented evidence gap under the registered v1 search universe.**

The pass condition is **not** that joint costs were shown to be zero. It is that a dedicated, predeclared search for intrinsic A+D allocation/construction evidence was completed to the stopping rule, and the resulting evidence state is now fixed.

## Strict target

Eligible evidence had to measure an additional direct resource, energetic, biosynthetic, construction, opportunity, or intrinsic fitness cost associated with **simultaneous investment** in:

```text
A = a declared floral attraction/signal/reward axis
D = a distinct independently justified flower-specific defence/access axis
```

Ecological pollinator interference, trait covariance, whole-plant defence cost, attraction cost alone, defence cost alone, agent-induced resource-reallocation inference, and a single material serving both reward and defence were excluded by `JOINT_COST_SEARCH_PROTOCOL_V1.md`.

## Search backbone

The registered families were:

```text
JC01 floral attraction defence allocation cost
JC02 flower signal resistance tradeoff allocation
JC03 floral display chemical defence covariance resource cost
```

Recall expansions included construction cost, energetic cost, carbon/nitrogen allocation, biosynthetic/metabolic cost, resource budget, opportunity cost, nectar/scent/petal cost, floral-defence cost, and physiological trade-off.

## Expansion results

### Batch 1

The search surfaced several empirically important but non-identifying architectures:

- experimental evolution under pollination × herbivory, where reduced evolution of floral attraction is interpreted as likely resource reallocation (`Ramos & Schiestl 2019`);
- herbivory-induced changes in floral signalling, where allocation and hormonal cross-talk remain alternative mechanisms (`Schiestl 2014`);
- comparative integration of floral scent and defensive chemistry (`Thosteman et al. 2024`);
- classic resistance-cost experiments where D has fitness costs but no distinct floral A+D budget is measured.

```text
strict joint-cost studies: 0
new exclusion/design classes: yes
```

### Batch 2

The search added:

- nectar-secondary-metabolite systems in which allocation/transport costs are discussed rather than measured (`Manson et al. 2012, 2013`);
- marginal whole-plant defence-cost experiments;
- phenolic-nectar behavioural studies suggesting that defensive chemistry might allow lower sugar expenditure, without measuring plant expenditure (`Liu et al. 2007`).

```text
strict joint-cost studies: 0
new exclusion/design classes: yes
```

The Manson 2013 paper is itself useful historical context: it notes the lack of direct tests of allocation costs for nectar secondary metabolites while distinguishing those hypothetical physiological costs from the ecological consequences it actually measures.

### Batch 3

A direct reward-cost experiment in `Dalechampia` showed that resin reward production can reduce seed production. The resin also has an ancestral defensive function, making this an unusually close conceptual case.

It still fails the strict joint-cost target because reward and defence are functions of the **same material**, rather than simultaneous investment in two distinct A and D axes. The batch also found fine-scale flower-defence allocation studies (`Lomatia`, `Nicotiana`) that quantify where D is deployed but not the added cost of jointly producing A and D.

```text
strict joint-cost studies: 0
new exclusion/design classes: yes
```

### Batch 4

A non-identical recall expansion focused on energetic/construction cost, flower-specific defence and resource reallocation. Hits mapped to already adjudicated classes:

- agent-induced or selection-inferred resource reallocation;
- flower-specific D allocation without A joint cost;
- chemical integration/regulatory partition without expenditure accounting.

```text
strict joint-cost studies: 0
new eligible joint-cost design classes: 0
```

This is the first consecutive no-new-design batch.

### Batch 5

A final expansion targeted direct floral signal + defensive compound + fitness/resource cost combinations. Hits again mapped to existing classes:

- direct A/reward cost only;
- marginal D cost;
- ecological pollinator interference;
- trait integration/covariance;
- single-material dual function;
- single-D multiconsumer ecology.

```text
strict joint-cost studies: 0
new eligible joint-cost design classes: 0
```

This is the second consecutive no-new-design batch. The preregistered stopping rule is satisfied.

## Strict result

```text
strict direct measured A+D allocation/construction-cost studies: 0
strict kappa estimate:                                          0 available estimates
kappa identified empirically:                                  no
kappa estimated as zero:                                       no
```

The most important distinction is between:

```text
ecological cost
    D changes pollinator behaviour or pollination
    -> belongs to pollinator-interference / other ecological channels

marginal physiological cost
    producing A alone or D alone consumes resources
    -> biologically real but does not identify the A×D joint curvature

joint intrinsic cost
    additional cost depends on simultaneous A and distinct flower-specific D investment
    -> the strict kappa target; not identified in the saturated v1 search
```

## Scientific interpretation

The bounded evidence-gap statement is:

> Across the registered joint-cost search, studies commonly infer trade-offs from selection treatments, quantify marginal attraction or defence costs, test phenotypic integration, or measure ecological interference. We did not identify a study that directly accounts for the intrinsic additional cost of simultaneously producing a distinct floral attraction trait and a flower-specific defence/access trait.

This is a statement about the saturated registered search universe, not proof that such a cost is biologically absent or that no future study can identify it.

## Theory consequence

The empirical synthesis must carry `kappa` as **unidentified**, not zero.

Separate A costs and D costs cannot be summed or multiplied to manufacture `kappa`. Weak A–D covariance cannot calibrate it. The theoretical sensitivity analysis remains the appropriate way to show how conclusions change across plausible `kappa` values until a direct empirical design exists.
