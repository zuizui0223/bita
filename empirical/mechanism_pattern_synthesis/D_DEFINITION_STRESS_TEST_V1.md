# D-definition stress test v1

## Why this audit is necessary

The mechanism-first theory defines `D` operationally as one flower-specific trait with a demonstrated antagonist-reduction role. The manuscript sometimes abbreviates this as floral defence/access limitation.

The strongest direct crossed-trait evidence, Kessler et al. 2015, uses floral nectar production. To orient this factor in the theory, `D` is defined as **nectar restriction** (greater D = lower nectar). Nectar restriction reduces Manduca sexta oviposition and therefore passes the broad operational antagonist-reduction gate.

However, nectar restriction is not a conventional positive-investment defence trait. Treating it as interchangeable with toxins, barriers, sticky surfaces, waxes, bracts, or water calyces would overstate the biological scope of the direct factorial evidence.

## Three D scopes

### D-broad: antagonist-reducing floral state/access axis

Any flower-specific axis whose oriented increase reduces antagonist use or damage.

Under this scope, Kessler 2015 qualifies strongly:

- factor is floral nectar availability/restriction;
- flower number was standardized for oviposition assays;
- non-flowering controls showed no line differences in oviposition;
- nectar loss sharply reduced M. sexta oviposition.

### D-functional: evolved floral trait with demonstrated antagonist-reduction function

Requires a trait state to have a source-supported protective/access-limiting role, but need not be a costly defensive investment.

Kessler 2015 is plausible but interpretation is less direct because the paper frames nectar mainly as reward and its reduction as avoidance of herbivore cost, not as a named defensive adaptation.

### D-narrow: conventional floral defence investment

Chemical toxin/deterrent, structural barrier, sticky/slippery surface, protective bract/calyx, or another positively expressed protective trait.

Kessler 2015 does **not** satisfy this narrow scope. The direct factorial is therefore not a direct test of attraction × conventional floral defence investment.

## Consequence for U7

The direct-factorial evidence must be reported with scope labels:

```text
broad operational D:     Kessler 2015 is a high-specificity direct A x D test
functional D:            admissible but requires careful wording
narrow defence D:        direct sign-switch evidence remains sparse/unresolved
```

Kessler 2008 nicotine is closer to narrow chemical defence but intervention specificity is weaker because nicotine silencing is systemic.

Impatiens remains coefficient-level evidence but sign unresolved.

## Falsification implication

The effective-domain separation rule should not depend on expanding the word 'defence' after seeing a convenient result. Therefore two versions of the universality claim must be kept distinct:

1. **general interaction-filtering claim** — attraction × antagonist-reducing floral access/defence axis;
2. **narrow floral-defence claim** — attraction × positively expressed conventional defence trait.

The first currently has direct sign-switch support from Kessler 2015. The second is supported strongly by recurrent marginal/matched mechanisms but has much weaker direct crossed-trait identification.

## Promotion gate added

Do not finalize the paper-level conclusion until one of the following is defensibly chosen:

- explicitly broaden the theory and title language to `defence/access limitation`, accepting Kessler 2015 as direct evidence; or
- retain narrow `defence` language and downgrade Kessler 2015 to an adjacent direct-factorial analogue rather than a strict narrow-defence test.

This is a scientific-definition decision, not copyediting.
