# Aquilegia 2013 inflorescence-trichome context audit v1

## Source

Jaime R, Rey PJ, Alcántara JM, Bastida JM. **Glandular trichomes as an inflorescence defence mechanism against insect herbivores in Iberian columbines.** *Oecologia* 172:1051–1060. DOI `10.1007/s00442-012-2553-z`.

The primary article reports manipulative trichome-removal experiments in eight populations spanning four subspecies of *Aquilegia vulgaris* and *A. pyrenaica*.

## Strong biological result

Glandular trichomes on the reproductive inflorescence trap small phytophagous insects. Experimental loss of trichome protection:

- reduced the number of insects trapped by the inflorescence;
- increased herbivore damage to flowers and fruits;
- reduced healthy fruit set;
- produced stronger effects in taxa/populations with denser glandular trichomes.

At population level, herbivore abundance was positively correlated with glandular-trichome density, and the damage increase following trichome removal was greater in populations with higher trichome density.

This is strong evidence that sticky reproductive-module surfaces can act as adaptive defence.

## Why it is NOT added to the focal route ledger

The active paper defines `D` as a declared **flower-specific** antagonist-reducing trait. In the Aquilegia experiment, the central measured/removed glandular trichomes include the inflorescence stalk/pedicel reproductive module rather than an unambiguously flower-only petal/corolla surface.

Promoting this study as a canonical `D -> antagonism` record would silently broaden the organ definition of `D` from flower-specific to inflorescence-level defence.

That expansion is not needed for the current theory and is therefore rejected.

## Pattern value retained

The study remains useful as reproductive-module context because it independently shows:

```text
sticky/glandular reproductive surfaces can reduce herbivore access
defence efficacy varies among populations and taxa
higher local herbivore pressure covaries with denser reproductive defensive structures
loss of reproductive-module defence can reduce fruit set
```

These observations strengthen population/context dependence of defensive efficacy but are not counted in the  route-ledger cluster total.

## Inference boundary

Not admitted:

```text
inflorescence-stalk trichomes = focal floral D
fruit-set effect = W_AD
population correlation = evidence of prevalence
four subspecies / eight populations = independent route N
```
