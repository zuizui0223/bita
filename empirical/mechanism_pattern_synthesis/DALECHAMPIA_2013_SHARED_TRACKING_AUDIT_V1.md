# Dalechampia scandens 2013 shared-tracking audit v1

## Source

Pérez-Barrales R, Bolstad GH, Pélabon C, Hansen TF, Armbruster WS. **Pollinators and seed predators generate conflicting selection on Dalechampia blossoms.** *Oikos* 122:1411–1428. DOI `10.1111/j.1600-0706.2013.20780.x`.

The primary source reports natural variation in showy bract area, resin-gland size/reward, pollinator visitation, pollen receipt, seed production, and seed predation, followed by multivariate fitness modelling.

## Pattern value

The showy involucral bract is a clear attraction/display axis `A` in the focal system.

The same bract axis is tracked by both mutualists and antagonists:

```text
larger bracts -> more bee visitation / pollen receipt
larger bracts -> more seed-predator attack
```

This is a visual/morphological replication of the current same-signal shared-tracking Pattern, which until now is anchored largely by scent/volatile systems.

## Pollinator pathway

The primary article reports that bees preferentially visited larger-bracted blossoms.

Source-reported scale translations include:

```text
+1 SD bract area (~96.4 mm^2; 28.3% of mean)
patch visitation probability: 83.1% -> 94.4%
within-patch visitation probability: +7.6 percentage points
pollen load on stigmas: +49.9%
```

The article also estimated positive pollinator-mediated directional selection on bract size.

Pattern state:

```text
A -> pollination: positive tracking
```

## Seed-predator pathway

Seed weevils also preferentially attacked blossoms with larger bracts.

The primary source reports that a +1 SD increase in bract area increased predicted seed predation from approximately 0.2% to 2.5%, with a very steep nonlinear increase toward the largest bracts in the observed population.

The fitness decomposition estimated strong negative selection on larger bracts generated by seed predators, opposing pollinator-mediated selection.

Pattern state:

```text
A -> antagonism: positive tracking / increased antagonist exposure
```

## Same-system meaning

This study provides a particularly clean instance of **shared tracking of one attraction/display trait by mutualists and antagonists**.

It should not be described merely as generic 'conflicting selection'. For the Mechanism → Pattern paper, the important observation is that increasing display value can simultaneously open both the mutualist and antagonist channels.

The resulting net selection on bract size tended toward stabilizing because the two agents imposed opposing fitness consequences.

## Inference boundary

This paper does NOT supply:

```text
a separate defence trait D
D -> pollination
D -> antagonism
direct A x D interaction
rho / iota / kappa
```

The seed-predator effect on `A` cannot be relabelled as defence.

The 2012 Dalechampia reward-cost study (`10.1093/aob/mcs091`) is useful linked context but does not create a defence axis either; it found no detectable seed-production cost of experimentally increasing or decreasing resin secretion.

## Relationship to existing Pattern clusters

Theis et al. 2014 shows the same floral sesquiterpenoid axis associated with specialist pollinator and floral-herbivore use.

Dalechampia 2013 provides an independent plant lineage, signal modality, and antagonist guild:

```text
Theis 2014:       scent -> squash bee + cucumber beetle
Dalechampia 2013: visual bract -> resin bee + seed weevil
```

Convergence therefore strengthens **shared attraction-signal tracking** as a cross-system Pattern class rather than a scent-specific phenomenon.
