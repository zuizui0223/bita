# Mechanism-first universality validation v9

## Purpose

Re-test the strongest v8 conclusion with an explicit falsification stance. The goal is not manuscript synchronization. It is to decide whether one universal switching rule has actually been identified strongly enough to stop scientific work.

## Result: the v8 synthesis was too compressed

The evidence currently supports **two related but not yet fully unified empirical results**.

### Result A — defence-side effective-domain separation is strongly recurrent

For a flower-specific antagonist-reducing D trait, the qualitative state of the D -> antagonist and D -> pollinator routes is repeatedly associated with whether antagonist and pollinator channels experience the same effective defence/access domain.

Independent implementations include:

- consumer susceptibility and chemical dose;
- cumulative exposure;
- attack route;
- floral spatial geometry;
- developmental timing;
- visitor functional mode.

Boundary cases also align:

- broad overlap can produce pollinator interference (`Bejaria`);
- antagonist bypass can make the focal D route fail (`Salvia`-type bypass cases);
- defence-like chemistry without demonstrated antagonist reduction is not admitted as D (`Lupinus` boundary).

This result survives explicit adverse-case testing and is currently the strongest candidate for a **conditional universality rule on the defence side**.

### Result B — direct crossed A x D sign is heterogeneous across ecological context

Direct or near-direct crossed designs show that the attraction-defence interaction sign is not fixed.

- `Impatiens`: coefficient-level interactions are sign-unresolved and reproductive-component dependent.
- Kessler 2008 `Nicotiana`: a lower-specificity crossed BA x nicotine design gives a positive sign-robust descriptive factorial signal.
- Kessler 2015 `Nicotiana`: a high-specificity crossed floral BA x nectar-production design changes discrete interaction sign across pollinator contexts.

With `D` oriented as nectar restriction, source-reported seed means yield:

```text
native visitor community: Delta_AD = -0.7900
Manduca sexta:            Delta_AD = -0.4320
Hyles lineata:            Delta_AD = +0.8699
```

Thus **direct sign heterogeneity is real at source-mean level**.

## Critical correction: Result B is not yet mechanistically explained by Result A

The single-pollinator seed-set assays in Kessler 2015 isolate pollination service. They do not expose the same experimental seed outcome to simultaneous antagonist damage. The sign reversal therefore cannot be claimed as a direct demonstration that antagonist-pollinator effective-domain separation generates the total A x D sign switch.

The source itself provides a plausible independent mechanism for the pollinator-context sign change:

```text
Manduca sexta: scent and nectar are both important for outcrossing
Hyles lineata: either scent or nectar alone is sufficient; loss of both is strongly detrimental
```

This is cue/reward redundancy and pollinator-specific response architecture. It is compatible with Part I's general channel-balance logic, but it is **not identical to the defence-side effective-domain separation rule**.

The field oviposition experiment separately shows that nectar absence strongly reduces `M. sexta` oviposition and that floral scent also affects oviposition. Non-flowering controls do not show line differences, supporting a floral route. However, the published design does not provide a single common fitness outcome in which pollination benefit, antagonist loss, and their A x D interactions are jointly decomposed with uncertainty.

## Prospective-style coding check for Kessler 2015

Using source information that does not use the seed-set A x D contrast itself:

### Pollinator response architecture

- `M. sexta`: high dependence on BA and nectar -> strong mutualist-domain overlap between the two floral axes.
- `H. lineata`: low dependence on either single axis, strong failure only when both are absent -> redundancy / compensatory mutualist architecture.

### Antagonist response architecture

- `M. sexta` oviposition: nectar absence strongly reduces egg laying; scent absence also reduces egg laying more weakly.

Predicted implication from Part I:

- changing pollinator identity should change `iota` / mutualist interaction architecture;
- therefore the total A x D sign can change even if the antagonist side is held conceptually fixed.

Observed source-mean sign reversal is consistent with this broad channel-balance prediction.

What is **not** identified is whether a single scalar 'domain separation' measure predicts the direct sign.

## External stress tests

Independent ecological-agent factorials (`Helleborus`, `Fragaria`, `Trifolium`) show that pollinator- and herbivore-mediated fitness/selection effects depend on one another. These studies independently support context-dependent channel balance, but they do not cross a validated floral A and flower-specific D and therefore cannot close the mechanistic bridge.

## Current falsification status

### Supported strongly

1. attraction signals are not mutualist-exclusive across modalities;
2. flower-specific antagonist-reducing traits recur across chemical and physical implementations;
3. D -> pollinator effects are not fixed;
4. defence-side route state is repeatedly organized by effective consumer-domain separation/overlap/bypass;
5. direct crossed A x D sign is not universal and can change with pollinator context.

### Not yet established

1. that **one** effective-domain scalar or classification predicts the sign of total `W_AD` across direct crossed designs;
2. that the Kessler 2015 sign reversal is caused by antagonist-pollinator domain separation rather than pollinator cue/reward architecture alone;
3. a channel-specific empirical decomposition into `rho`, `iota`, and `kappa`;
4. any empirical estimate of `kappa = C_AD`.

## Revised universality statement

The strongest statement currently supported is:

> The universal candidate is not a fixed attraction-defence sign. Instead, the constituent interaction channels recur, and their realised states are repeatedly filtered by consumer-specific susceptibility, access, timing, dose, and functional response. Defence-side effective-domain separation is a strong recurrent conditional rule; direct crossed A x D experiments independently show sign heterogeneity across consumer context. Whether these two observations can be reduced to one predictive empirical switching rule for total W_AD remains unresolved.

## Next decisive tests

1. Build an **outcome-blind moderator coding** for every admissible matched-D and direct-factorial system: consumer identity, dose/exposure, access overlap, attack mode, temporal overlap, pollinator dependence on D, antagonist dependence on A/D.
2. Ask whether these pre-coded moderators predict guarded/interference/bypass states better than broad labels such as chemical/physical or taxon.
3. For direct A x D systems, test whether pollinator dependence on D and antagonist dependence on A/D predict the observed interaction sign; do not use the sign itself when defining moderators.
4. Search only for additional crossed designs that provide both pollinator and antagonist channel outcomes under the same A x D manipulation. Do not reopen generic literature hunting.
5. Keep U8 frozen as a design gap unless a true channel-specific `C_AD` estimate appears.
